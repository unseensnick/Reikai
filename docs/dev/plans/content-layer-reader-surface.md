# Content-layer reader surface (one host, two providers)

> **Standing rules for every session working this surface.** The program rules bind here and are not restated: they live in [.claude/rules/content-layer.md](../../../.claude/rules/content-layer.md), which loads every session (write-once, typed capability slots over nullable soup, derive-or-do-not-own, the pin-once ladder, decline expiry). For the program context read [content-layer-architecture.md](content-layer-architecture.md) and [unified-content-ui.md](unified-content-ui.md) first. Two more bind on this surface specifically. **The viewer classes are never modified**: `PagerViewer`, `WebtoonViewer` and `WebGpuViewer` stay byte-identical to upstream, and a step that wants to edit one has stopped being this takeover and become the reverted Option A below. And **position is a typed capability, never an `Int`**: manga reads a page index, novels read a fraction, and any shared code that wants a page number is asking the wrong question. The reader is the only surface in the program whose host is an Activity, so the usual "shared engine above two live models" shape bends here in one specific way, described under the amendment.

## Goal

One Reikai-owned reader orchestration serving manga and light novels from a single host, with the manga and novel engines staying separate underneath it. Novels move off the bespoke WebView plus vendored LNReader `core.js` reader onto a native text viewer lifted from tsundoku, entering the same host, the same chrome, the same chapter navigator and the same settings as manga.

**Seamless chapter-to-chapter reading is a delivered capability, not a follow-up.** Scrolling out of one chapter straight into the next, the way the manga webtoon viewer already does, was one of the original reasons for the migration and it ships with it.

This is the sixth surface of the content-layer program ([content-layer-architecture.md](content-layer-architecture.md)) and its fourth orchestration takeover, after the library, the migrate flow and the recents feeds. It runs next, ahead of the downloads unification (Road B), which closes the program (owner, 2026-09-03). It supersedes [unified-reader.md](unified-reader.md), whose chrome-only scope was ruled before the program's seam was settled, and absorbs the engine choice from [novel-reader-tsundoku.md](novel-reader-tsundoku.md), whose "fold novels into `ReaderActivity` the way tsundoku does" framing does not survive this design.

## Why

**The behaviour is forked, and the fork is total.** There is no shared reader orchestration at all today. Manga runs `ReaderActivity` plus `ReaderViewModel`; novels run a Voyager screen plus `NovelReaderScreenModel`, the last model in the app still on `ScreenModel` and still resolving eighteen types through Injekt. Every reader behaviour exists twice: chapter navigation, progress, mark-as-read, history, tracking, download-ahead, delete-after-read, orientation, keep-screen-on, brightness, and the settings sheet. The shipped chrome sharing reaches two composables (`ReaderContentOverlay` and `ReaderTopBar`); `ReaderAppBars`, `ReaderBottomBar`, `ChapterNavigator` and `ReaderPageIndicator` remain manga-only.

**The sync cost is the highest of any surface taken over so far, and the divergence is the lowest.** Twelve months of upstream history at the synced base: `ReaderActivity.kt` took 20 commits (+321/-344), `ReaderViewModel.kt` 15 (+201/-169), the whole `ui/reader/` tree 42, the viewer package another 20, and `presentation/reader/` 16. Against that, Reikai's `ReaderActivity` is 1020 lines to upstream's 944 with 16 `// RK` markers, and `ReaderViewModel` 1339 to 1022 with 46. **This inverts the library takeover's justification and the amendment does not borrow it.** The library was ruled partly because its sync tax was already sunk (1076 lines to upstream's 837, 76 markers, 851 diverging lines), so every sync was already a hand-merge. The reader host still merges close to mechanically. The case here rests entirely on the duplicate-implementation cost of two reader hosts, which is the failure mode the 2026-08-06 ruling exists to remove, and on the fact that the novel half is being rebuilt regardless: its engine is a vendored JS file nobody can update.

**The user-facing prize is real and already asked for.** `unseensnick/Reikai#55` asks for a reader settings surface that does not require hunting, and the app-level Settings > Reader screen is 68 rows with novel content scattered across three non-adjacent groups distinguished only by a title suffix. Novels also gain the whole manga chrome they lack today: the chapter navigator, the page-actions surface where it applies, and one settings vocabulary.

## Approach

### The amendment: scope and hard lines

This is the sixth orchestration amendment to the program, recorded alongside the others in [content-layer-architecture.md](content-layer-architecture.md). It supersedes the reader row of the seam-depth table in [.claude/rules/content-layer.md](../../../.claude/rules/content-layer.md), which read "chrome only, the two engines are separate by design". That ruling predates the program's settlement and was made when chrome sharing was the whole ambition.

- **The host stays one Activity, and that is what makes this surface different.** Every previous takeover put a shared engine above two live per-type models. A host that owns a window, system bars and an orientation lock cannot exist twice, so `ReaderActivity` remains the single host for both content types, keeping the responsibilities only an Activity can own: system bars and insets, cutout padding, orientation, keep-screen-on, custom brightness, the secure flag, the brightness and colour overlay, menu visibility, key and motion dispatch, the loading and error dialogs, and the activity transitions. All of that is already content-neutral in current code.
- **The takeover owns orchestration**: a Reikai-owned reader engine holding chapter navigation, position, app-bar and navigator state, dialog dispatch and the action verbs, dispatching to a provider per content type. The shared chrome composables (`ReaderAppBars`, `ReaderBottomBar`, `ChapterNavigator`, `ReaderPageIndicator`) become content-neutral and move under the engine.
- **`ReaderViewModel` stays live as the manga provider**, minimally patched, and is not manifested. That is this amendment's ruling, not an inherited default: the churn measured above gives no case for absorbing it, and the same clause was ruled the same way for `LibraryViewModel`, `UpdatesViewModel` and `HistoryViewModel`.
- **The engine floor stays Mihon and synced**, never reimplemented: `ChapterLoader` and every `PageLoader`, `DownloadManager`, `SetReadStatus`, `UpsertHistory`, `TrackChapter`, `UpdateChapter`, and every interactor below them. The novel sinks (`SetNovelReadStatus`, `UpsertNovelHistory`, `TrackNovelChapter`, `NovelDownloadManager`, `NovelChapterRepository`) stay likewise. A step that starts reimplementing what `setReadStatus` or `ChapterLoader` does has gone too far.
- **The viewers are never modified.** `PagerViewer`, `WebtoonViewer`, `WebGpuViewer` and their support classes stay byte-identical to upstream and keep porting mechanically. The novel text viewer is a third implementation of the existing five-member `Viewer` interface, added beside them, not a change to it. If the interface itself must grow a member, that is a design decision recorded here first, not an incidental edit.
- **`NovelReaderScreenModel` and the WebView reader dissolve entirely.** Unlike the other surfaces' novel models, this one is not reshaped into a provider: its engine is a WebView plus a vendored `core.js` that is being replaced, so the novel provider is net-new code over the existing novel interactors, and the old tree is deleted and manifested.
- **Replaced Mihon files are deleted and manifested in the same commit**, which the `pre-commit` hook enforces. Expect very few rows: almost everything on this surface is engine or host, so the usual delete-and-manifest texture does not apply and most of the change lands as live `// RK` islands instead.
- **Two upstreams from here on.** Tsundoku becomes a second sync source for the novel viewer only, credited as an Apache-2.0 port the way the Komikku ports are, and recorded in [feature-ports.md](../feature-ports.md) rather than the base ledger. `scripts/off-path-check.ps1` is built around one base; extending it is part of the final step, not an assumption.

### Ruled design picks (owner, 2026-09-03)

- **Shape C, the neutral host, over the two alternatives.** Folding novels into `ReaderViewModel` by synthesizing manga-shaped rows was rejected: it reintroduces novels-as-manga at the reader boundary, which the program rejected at the data layer for reasons that do not stop being true one layer up. A second Reikai-owned reader host was rejected as the shape that permanently guarantees two of everything, which is what this program exists to stop.
- **Position is a sealed type answered by the provider, and it names its chapter.** Manga answers a page index and a count; novels answer a fraction, which is what `novel_chapters.last_text_progress` already stores as hundredths. The chapter component is not optional detail: because the viewer holds more than one chapter at a time for seamless scrolling, a position that says only "62%" cannot say 62% of what, and the host's existing `onPageSelected(page)` already carries `page.chapter` for exactly this reason. The navigator and indicator read a derived fraction and label scoped to the visible chapter.
- **The novel provider is proven with a deliberately plain viewer before the tsundoku renderer is lifted**, so a host-plumbing failure and a rendering failure cannot arrive together.
- **Our TTS transport is kept and tsundoku's text handling is taken.** Measured rather than assumed: we have a `MediaSessionCompat` with lock-screen and headset transport that tsundoku has nowhere in its tree, and tsundoku has an utterance splitter capped at the engine's maximum input length with a no-spaces branch for CJK, which we lack entirely. Neither side requests audio focus, ducks, or has a sleep timer, and neither highlights below paragraph granularity, so the roadmap's in-text-highlight item survives this migration rather than being delivered by it.
- **Seamless chapter-to-chapter scrolling comes from Mihon's mechanism, not tsundoku's.** The host already spans chapters: `WebtoonAdapter.setChapters` builds one item list from the previous, current and next chapters' pages with `ChapterTransition` items between them, `WebtoonViewer` calls `requestPreloadChapter` as a boundary approaches, and `onPageSelected` reports which chapter is now visible through `ReaderPage.chapter`. A text viewer rendering `ViewerChapters` the same way inherits all of it, so novels get the capability from the engine rather than from a second implementation. **Tsundoku's own infinite scroll is deliberately not ported:** it lives inside their host-coupled `NovelViewer` (a `loadedChapters` list, their own append path, threshold auto-load, cross-chapter progress math, and four `prepare*ChapterForInfiniteScroll` methods on their forked `ReaderViewModel`), it is gated off by default there, and porting it would rebuild what the host already provides while dragging in the fork this takeover exists to avoid.
- **The settings restructure ships first, before the reader work starts.** It is independent of the migration, it is the visible answer to `unseensnick/Reikai#55`, and it is the one piece a user notices immediately.

### What this must not become

**Option A was built here and reverted.** A single Compose shell hosting both mediums, with the image viewers decoupled from `ReaderActivity` behind a `ViewerHost` interface and embedded through `AndroidView`, was built across two commits and reverted. The record in [unified-reader.md](unified-reader.md) is explicit that the revert was cost, not feasibility: an on-device spike confirmed the webtoon view scrolled and zoomed correctly inside `AndroidView`. The cost was that it diverged the hot image-viewer files from upstream, creating a permanent hand-port tax, and ran modified viewers even on the fallback path.

That decline does not transfer to this takeover, and the reason is precise enough to be a test: Option A's tax came from decoupling the **viewers**, and this takeover neutralizes the **orchestration** above them while leaving the viewers untouched. That is the same line every previous amendment drew. It also means the decline has not expired so much as been routed around, and the routing only holds while the viewers-are-never-modified rule holds. The moment a step edits a viewer, this surface has become Option A and the revert's reasoning applies again in full.

### Known defects and traps in the surfaces being replaced

Recorded so the takeover fixes them deliberately rather than carrying them across, each naming the step that owns it.

- **Five places assume an integer page index** (step 1). The chapter navigator builds its slider as `steps = totalPages - 2` over `valueRange = 1f..totalPages`, so a chapter modelled as one stub page collapses the range and produces negative steps. `moveToPageIndex` resolves a page by list index. `updateChapterProgress` persists `lastPageRead` as an index and fires completion only when `pages.lastIndex == pageIndex`, so a fraction-driven viewer would never mark a chapter read. Download-ahead uses the ratio `page.number / pages.size`. Restoring after process death assigns `requestedPage` an index.
- **A long paragraph is silently truncated when read aloud** (step 5). `SystemTtsEngine.speak` passes the whole paragraph to `TextToSpeech.speak` with no length check, and the platform drops text past its maximum input length. Tsundoku's splitter is the fix and arrives with the renderer.
- **`MainActivity` implements the reader's `NovelVolumeKeyHost`** (step 6), a compile-level dependency of a Mihon file on the novel reader package, which volume keys routed through `Viewer.handleKeyEvent` remove.
- **The TTS transport has exactly one owner and it is being deleted** (step 5). `NovelTtsSession`'s callbacks are written only by `NovelTtsController`, which is constructed only by `NovelReaderScreenModel`. Replacing the model compiles clean and leaves the lock-screen and notification buttons as silent no-ops.
- **The reader settings suffix carries search breadcrumbs** (step 8). `contentTypedCategory` exists so a duplicated setting reads distinctly and its settings-search breadcrumb differs per content type, so retiring the suffix is a search regression risk, not only a cosmetic change.
- **Nothing on this surface is tested** (every step). No test class touches the novel reader, and the repo has no Compose UI tests, so every check below is a device check or a new unit test written against a kernel that did not previously exist.

## Sequenced steps

Both readers work at every point. No step starts before the previous one has passed its check on device.

0. **Record the ruling.** This doc, the amendment row in [content-layer-architecture.md](content-layer-architecture.md), the seam-depth table flip in [.claude/rules/content-layer.md](../../../.claude/rules/content-layer.md), the supersede note on [unified-reader.md](unified-reader.md) and the absorb note on [novel-reader-tsundoku.md](novel-reader-tsundoku.md), and the roadmap lines. *Verify: `docs-lint` and the `pre-commit` hooks pass, and the seam table and this doc state the same depth in the same words.*
1. **The settings restructure**, taken out of sequence because it is independent and user-visible. Detail, sequencing and its own checks live in [settings-restructure.md](settings-restructure.md), which the owner widened on 2026-09-03 into a sanctioned rewrite covering About and a Sources consolidation as well as the Reader screen.
2. **The typed position, manga only.** The sealed chapter-scoped position type, the provider answering it, and the navigator, indicator and app bars reading a derived fraction and label. Exercise it on the webtoon viewer, not only the pager, since webtoon is the one that already spans chapters and is therefore the case a novel viewer will look like. *Verify: a unit test over the position kernel, watched failing against the old code first, plus a device pass that the manga seekbar scrubs to the same pages, shows the same numbers, still marks a chapter read on the last page, and still reports the right chapter in the top bar when scrolling across a webtoon chapter boundary.*
3. **The engine skeleton over one provider.** The Reikai-owned engine takes navigation, position, app-bar state and dialog dispatch, with `ReaderViewModel` beneath it as the sole provider. Anything the engine failed to carry breaks while there is exactly one implementation to compare against. *Verify: the manga reader walked from all six entry points (library, details, updates, the notification action, the deep link, page preview), plus a written list of what moved into the engine with each item marked present, deliberately dropped, or missing.*
4. **The provider contract and a plain novel viewer, single chapter.** The novel provider over the existing novel interactors, rendering the current HTML pipeline behind the `Viewer` interface, showing one chapter at a time. Seamless scrolling is deliberately held back one step so a plumbing failure and a multi-chapter failure cannot arrive together. *Verify: open a novel from the library, read it, change chapter, close and reopen to the same place, with progress and history confirmed by driving the UI rather than reading the database on the Fold.*
5. **Seamless chapter-to-chapter scrolling.** The novel viewer renders `ViewerChapters` across previous, current and next as `WebtoonAdapter` does, honours `requestPreloadChapter` at the boundaries, and reports the visible chapter so the top bar, progress, mark-as-read, history and tracking all follow the reader across the seam. *Verify: read from the middle of one chapter into the next without a load screen, confirm the top bar title changes at the boundary, the leaving chapter is marked read and gets a history row, and the arriving chapter's progress starts from the top rather than resuming a stale offset.*
6. **Lift the tsundoku renderer.** `NovelTextRenderer`, `NovelViewerSpans`, `NovelImageGetter`, `ChapterTextBlock` and the HTML and markdown helpers, swapped in behind an unchanged `Viewer` surface. *Verify: the same chapter side by side against the old WebView reader for paragraph spacing, indent, inline images, links, a CJK source, and a chapter long enough to exercise chunked precomputed text.*
7. **TTS re-host.** Keep `NovelTtsService` and `NovelTtsSession`, move the controller onto the new viewer, take the capped splitter, paragraph stepping and resume index, drop the JS half. *Verify: lock-screen and headset transport still work, a paragraph over the engine's maximum input length now reads to the end, and playback carries across a chapter boundary.*
8. **Volume keys.** Routed through `Viewer.handleKeyEvent`, `NovelVolumeKeyHost` removed from `MainActivity`. *Verify: volume keys scroll in the new reader, and `MainActivity` no longer imports the reader package.*
9. **Cut over and delete.** Repoint the three novel entry points, delete the WebView reader and the vendored `core.js`, manifest what qualifies, drop `voyager-screenModel` from the bundle, remove the nine dead `DomainModule` registrations and the sixteen interop entries. *Verify: `di-interop-check.ps1`, `DomainModuleTest`, and a minified `:app:assembleNightly` exercised on device, since trimming the Injekt tail is the R8 hazard class that has bitten twice.*
10. **Collapse the two in-reader settings sheets** into one with grouping and progressive disclosure, which the host makes possible for the first time. *Verify: every setting reachable for both content types, and a capability a type cannot support hidden rather than shown disabled.*
11. **The behaviour inventory.** Walk the replaced code end to end and mark every behaviour present, deliberately dropped with the reason, or missing. *Verify: the inventory is in this doc and every item is marked; an unmarked item means the takeover is not finished.*

## Decisions & tradeoffs

- **The host is one Activity and the engine sits above two providers.** The program's usual shape does not survive contact with a window-owning host, and pretending it does is how this surface would get mis-planned.
- **The viewers-never-modified rule is what keeps this from being Option A**, and it is the first thing to check when a step feels like it is fighting the design.
- **Position is typed, not an `Int`.** Sharing an integer page count between a paged medium and a continuous one is the derive-or-do-not-own defect in its most tempting form: the storage would be shared and the meaning forked.
- **The tsundoku port is the renderer, not the reader.** Their `NovelViewer` calls twenty-one distinct members of their forked `ReaderActivity`, so taking it whole would mean taking their fork of the file we are least willing to fork. The renderer underneath it is domain-free and is the part worth having.
- **We keep our TTS transport even though we are taking their engine.** The two halves were measured separately, and each side won one.
- **Accepted sync cost.** This is the highest-churn surface the program has taken over, on the least diverged files, and the amendment says so rather than borrowing the library's sunk-tax argument. Every future upstream reader fix becomes a manifest-guarded triage on the orchestration we own, while the viewers and the engine floor keep porting mechanically, which is the boundary that keeps the cost bounded.
- **The in-reader sheet collapse waits for the host** (step 9) rather than leading, because folding novels into the host changes what the two sheets are. The app-level settings screen does not wait, because it is orthogonal.
