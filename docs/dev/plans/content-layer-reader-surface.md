# Content-layer reader surface (one host, two providers)

> **Standing rules for every session working this surface.** The program rules bind here and are not restated: they live in [.claude/rules/content-layer.md](../../../.claude/rules/content-layer.md), which loads every session (write-once, typed capability slots over nullable soup, derive-or-do-not-own, the pin-once ladder, decline expiry). For the program context read [content-layer-architecture.md](content-layer-architecture.md) and [unified-content-ui.md](unified-content-ui.md) first. Three more bind on this surface specifically. **The image viewer classes are never diverged further than the divergences content-layer.md's Reader row enumerates**, which is the list that binds; the counting wording this doc used to carry was wrong twice and is not restated here. Four of them predate the takeover and the rest are owner-ruled islands, the chapter jump in `WebGpuViewer.setChapters` and its failed-load retry among them, each recorded below. The rule is that no step adds another. A step that wants to restructure one has stopped being this takeover and become the reverted Option A below. **Position is a typed capability, never an `Int`**, with one named exception: manga reads a page index, novels read a fraction, and the sole surviving integer is `ReaderChapter.requestedPage`, which the three image viewers read directly and which therefore stays exactly as it is (see "The restore path keeps its integer" below). And **the engine owns the viewer contract, not Mihon's `Viewer`**: `Viewer` becomes one adapter under a neutral contract rather than the interface a novel viewer implements, because it cannot carry a novel chapter without the mechanism this plan rejects (ruled, see Shape C below). The reader is the only surface in the program whose host is an Activity, so the usual "shared engine above two live models" shape bends here in one specific way, described under the amendment.

## Goal

One Reikai-owned reader orchestration serving manga and light novels from a single host, with the manga and novel engines staying separate underneath it. Novels move off the bespoke WebView plus vendored LNReader `core.js` reader onto **two rendering modes, a native text viewer ported from tsundoku and a WebView viewer that renders Reikai's own document**, entering the same host, the same chrome, the same chapter navigator and the same settings as manga.

**Both rendering modes ship, and the vendored JS goes either way** (owner, 2026-09-03). The native TextView mode is the default; the WebView mode exists because a text layout cannot render a source's own CSS faithfully, which is a capability some sources need, so it is a typed capability slot rather than redundancy. What is being replaced is not "a WebView" but an unmaintainable one: our reader runs 793 lines of verbatim-vendored LNReader `core.js` plus two minified third-party bundles (`van.js`, `text-vibe.js`) and a 91-line polyfill, none of which we can update. The native mode takes tsundoku's renderer and content pipeline, which have an upstream to sync from. The WebView mode is Reikai's own: `NovelWebDocument` builds the page and `assets/novel-web/reader.js` runs it, with the boundary and per-chapter progress half of tsundoku's `scroll-tracking.js` and their font-override rules ported into it (step 7 records what came across). `assets/lnreader-web/` is deleted whichever mode the user picks.

**Seamless chapter-to-chapter reading is a delivered capability, not a follow-up.** Scrolling out of one chapter straight into the next, the way the manga webtoon viewer already does, was one of the original reasons for the migration and it ships with it.

This is the sixth surface of the content-layer program ([content-layer-architecture.md](content-layer-architecture.md)) and its fourth orchestration takeover, after the library, the migrate flow and the recents feeds. It runs next, ahead of the downloads unification (Road B), which closes the program (owner, 2026-09-03). It supersedes [unified-reader.md](unified-reader.md), whose chrome-only scope was ruled before the program's seam was settled, and absorbs the engine choice from [novel-reader-tsundoku.md](novel-reader-tsundoku.md), whose "fold novels into `ReaderActivity` the way tsundoku does" framing does not survive this design.

## Why

**The behaviour is forked, and the fork is total.** There is no shared reader orchestration at all today. Manga runs `ReaderActivity` plus `ReaderViewModel`; novels run a Voyager screen plus `NovelReaderScreenModel`, the last model in the app still on `ScreenModel` and still resolving eighteen types through Injekt. Every reader behaviour exists twice: chapter navigation, progress, mark-as-read, history, tracking, download-ahead, delete-after-read, orientation, keep-screen-on, brightness, and the settings sheet. The shipped chrome sharing reaches two composables (`ReaderContentOverlay` and `ReaderTopBar`); `ReaderAppBars`, `ReaderBottomBar`, `ChapterNavigator` and `ReaderPageIndicator` remain manga-only.

**The sync cost is the highest of any surface taken over so far, and the divergence is the lowest.** Twelve months of upstream history at the synced base: `ReaderActivity.kt` took 20 commits (+321/-344), `ReaderViewModel.kt` 15 (+201/-169), the whole `ui/reader/` tree 42, the viewer package another 20, and `presentation/reader/` 16. Against that, Reikai's `ReaderActivity` is 1020 lines to upstream's 944 with 16 `// RK` markers, and `ReaderViewModel` 1339 to 1022 with 46. **This inverts the library takeover's justification and the amendment does not borrow it.** The library was ruled partly because its sync tax was already sunk (1076 lines to upstream's 837, 76 markers, 851 diverging lines), so every sync was already a hand-merge. The reader host still merges close to mechanically. The case here rests entirely on the duplicate-implementation cost of two reader hosts, which is the failure mode the 2026-08-06 ruling exists to remove, and on the fact that the novel half is being rebuilt regardless: its engine is a vendored JS file nobody can update.

**The user-facing prize is real and already asked for.** `unseensnick/Reikai#55` asks for a reader settings surface that does not require hunting, and the app-level Settings > Reader screen is 68 rows with novel content scattered across three non-adjacent groups distinguished only by a title suffix. Novels also gain the whole manga chrome they lack today: the chapter navigator, the page-actions surface where it applies, and one settings vocabulary.

## Approach

### The amendment: scope and hard lines

This is the sixth orchestration amendment to the program, recorded alongside the others in [content-layer-architecture.md](content-layer-architecture.md). It supersedes the reader row of the seam-depth table in [.claude/rules/content-layer.md](../../../.claude/rules/content-layer.md), which read "chrome only, the two engines are separate by design". That ruling predates the program's settlement and was made when chrome sharing was the whole ambition.

- **The host stays one Activity, and that is what makes this surface different.** Every previous takeover put a shared engine above two live per-type models. A host that owns a window, system bars and an orientation lock cannot exist twice, so `ReaderActivity` remains the single host for both content types, keeping the responsibilities only an Activity can own: system bars and insets, cutout padding, orientation, keep-screen-on, custom brightness, the secure flag, the brightness and colour overlay, menu visibility, key and motion dispatch, the loading and error dialogs, and the activity transitions. All of that is already content-neutral in current code.
- **The takeover owns orchestration**: a Reikai-owned reader engine holding chapter navigation, position, app-bar and navigator state, dialog dispatch and the action verbs, dispatching to a provider per content type. The shared chrome composables (`ReaderAppBars`, `ReaderBottomBar`, `ChapterNavigator`, `ReaderPageIndicator`) become content-neutral and move under the engine.
- **`ReaderViewModel` stays live as the manga provider**, minimally patched, and is not manifested. That is this amendment's ruling, not an inherited default: the churn measured above gives no case for absorbing it, and the same clause was ruled the same way for `LibraryViewModel`, `UpdatesViewModel` and `HistoryViewModel`.
- **The engine floor stays Mihon and synced**, never reimplemented: `ChapterLoader` and every `PageLoader`, `DownloadManager`, `SetReadStatus`, `UpsertHistory`, `TrackChapter`, `UpdateChapter`, and every interactor below them. The novel sinks (`SetNovelReadStatus`, `UpsertNovelHistory`, `TrackNovelChapter`, `NovelDownloadManager`, `NovelChapterRepository`) stay likewise. A step that starts reimplementing what `setReadStatus` or `ChapterLoader` does has gone too far.
- **The image viewers are not diverged further.** `PagerViewer`, `WebtoonViewer`, `WebGpuViewer` and their support classes keep porting mechanically, and no step adds a `// RK` marker beyond the mechanisms content-layer.md's Reader row enumerates. Four predate the takeover (the `seedColor` island in each viewer family, `WebtoonViewer`'s `volumeScrollDistance` and the two `handleKeyEvent` branches that use it). **The first ruled-in island was the chapter-jump fix in `WebGpuViewer.setChapters`** (owner, 2026-09-05), recorded under known defects below, for a live user-facing bug found while taking the surface over, and it is not a general licence. **A further ruled island, 2026-09-15**: `WebGpuViewer`'s failed-load retry, from the reader audit. A page that failed to load kept a progress ring forever and a failed neighbour chapter was refetched every 5 seconds with no way to retry; the owner chose an in-between of mihonapp/mihon#3813 (a tap retries) and Reikai's own islands over porting that open PR, which hit-tests taps against page geometry. Ours retries whatever failed is on screen, per the library's `ImagePage.isOnScreen`, from a tap in the menu region, and holds a failed neighbour until the next page turn. Verified on the emulator in paged mode with transition pages on; dual page, transition pages off and the long strip modes were not driven. The earlier wording, "byte-identical to upstream", was already false for two of the three files and is corrected here rather than quietly kept. `Viewer` itself is not modified either, because the two novel viewers sit behind the engine's neutral contract instead of implementing it; if `Viewer` must grow a member, that is a design decision recorded here first, not an incidental edit.
- **`NovelReaderScreenModel` and the vendored reader dissolve entirely.** Unlike the other surfaces' novel models, this one is not reshaped into a provider: its engine is a WebView plus a vendored `core.js` that is being replaced in **both** rendering modes, so the novel provider is net-new code over the existing novel interactors, and the old tree plus `assets/lnreader-web/` is deleted (all Reikai files, so no manifest rows). Keeping a WebView mode is not keeping this code.
- **Replaced Mihon files are deleted and manifested in the same commit**, which the `pre-commit` hook enforces. Expect very few rows: almost everything on this surface is engine or host, so the usual delete-and-manifest texture does not apply and most of the change lands as live `// RK` islands instead.
- **Two upstreams from here on.** Tsundoku becomes a second sync source for the native renderer, the shared layers beneath both viewers, and the few WebView-mode pieces step 7 took, credited as an Apache-2.0 port the way the Komikku ports are, and recorded in [feature-ports.md](../feature-ports.md) rather than the base ledger. Its row is written when the first port lands with a real SHA, not in advance. `scripts/off-path-check.ps1` is built around one base; extending it is part of the final step, not an assumption.
- **The restore path keeps its integer, and that is deliberate.** Reopening a chapter runs `Chapter.last_page_read` through `ChapterLoader` into `ReaderChapter.requestedPage`, which `PagerViewer`, `WebtoonViewer` and `WebGpuViewer` each read directly when they seek to the restored page. `ChapterLoader` is untouchable engine floor and the image viewers are not to be diverged, so the typed-position rule cannot reach this hop from either end. The resolution is that `requestedPage` stays exactly as it is and serves manga only, `ReaderChapter` gains a typed position beside it, and only the novel viewers read the typed one. Written down because the literal reading of "position is never an `Int`" leads straight into editing three viewers, which is the one thing that turns this takeover back into Option A.

### Ruled design picks (owner, 2026-09-03)

- **Shape C, the neutral host, over the two alternatives.** Folding novels into `ReaderViewModel` by synthesizing manga-shaped rows was rejected: it reintroduces novels-as-manga at the reader boundary, which the program rejected at the data layer for reasons that do not stop being true one layer up. A second Reikai-owned reader host was rejected as the shape that permanently guarantees two of everything, which is what this program exists to stop.
- **The engine owns a neutral viewer contract, and Mihon's `Viewer` is one adapter under it** (owner, 2026-09-03). The earlier framing, that a novel viewer is simply a third implementation of `Viewer`, does not survive contact with the interface. `Viewer.setChapters` takes a `ViewerChapters`, which is three `ReaderChapter`s, and a `ReaderChapter` wraps the manga `Chapter` from `data.database.models` with a secondary constructor over the manga domain `Chapter`. `ReaderPage` extends `Page(index, url, imageUrl, null)` with no text field, and `source-api`'s `Page` has none either. Tsundoku made it fit by adding `var text: String?` to `Page` plus `Source.fetchPageText`, which is exactly the novels-as-manga mechanism this plan rejects. Two other exits were weighed and declined: synthesizing a manga `Chapter` per novel chapter is that same mechanism at one remove, and generalizing `Viewer` / `ViewerChapters` / `ReaderPage` reaches into `source-api`, the contract installed extensions compile against, and forces edits in all three image viewers, which is the Option A tax. The neutral contract keeps the image viewers untouched and matches the program's adapters-are-the-only-seam rule, at the cost of one indirection in the host's viewer slot.
- **Two rendering modes, both from tsundoku, selected by a preference** (owner, 2026-09-03). Native TextView is the default and WebView is the alternative, mirroring tsundoku's own `novelRenderingMode`. The same preference is what makes steps 4 to 8 reachable: it gates the new reader against the old one during the build, defaulting to the old reader, and the old option is removed at the cutover. Tsundoku's two host-coupled viewers (`NovelViewer` 2091 lines, `NovelWebViewViewer` 2876) are **not** taken; our engine drives our own two viewers over their portable layers. The WebView half did not stay a port: step 7 built it on Reikai's own document, and its entry records what of tsundoku's `webview/` package came across.
- **Our own reader is not the base, and neither is a from-scratch renderer.** Keeping the LNReader-derived reader was rejected because its engine is the vendored `core.js` nobody can update, which is the whole reason this migration exists. Writing our own was rejected because text rendering is where the hard, already-solved problems are (precomputed text on long chapters, span performance, inline images, justification, CJK utterance splitting), and a from-scratch renderer has no upstream to sync from, which is the core of how Reikai is maintained. Tsundoku is a live Apache-2.0 fork whose portable layers total roughly 3850 lines of Kotlin plus 329 of readable JS, and it wins on both counts.
- **The settings the takeover carries, and the ones it defers** (owner, 2026-09-03). Font size, line height, the four margins, text alignment, font family and theme land inside the renderer lift, because they are the reader's visible identity and they have no UI outside the sheet being deleted. Paragraph indent, paragraph spacing, user regex replacements and custom fonts land too, at the pipeline-and-renderer step rather than the styling one, because indent and spacing are span concerns and regex replacements are a preprocessing concern. That absorbs the four items the roadmap's tsundoku feature-harvest line previously held, so that line narrows to what genuinely remains after the takeover. The full owed-settings inventory, including what silently loses its implementation, is the "Settings the takeover owes" section below.
- **The auto-mark-read threshold stays ours at 97%** (owner, 2026-09-03). It lived in the retired `NovelReaderScreenModel`'s `saveProgress` as `if (clamped >= 97)` and is `CONTINUOUS_COMPLETE_PERCENT` in the position kernel (`ReaderPosition.kt`); it comes from the LNReader lineage and is the behaviour users already have. Step 11 made it the default of a novel setting, `readerMarkReadPercent`, rather than a fixed number, so the kernel takes the percent a caller passes. Tsundoku's two answers are both declined: their shared `NovelProgress.DONE_THRESHOLD` of `0.99f` and their user-facing `novelMarkAsReadThreshold` slider defaulting to 95. The number moves into the typed position kernel as the novel side's completion predicate, because the file it lives in is deleted.
- **The six settings whose implementation dies with `core.js` are reimplemented, not hidden** (owner, 2026-09-03): bionic reading, remove extra spacing, tap edges to scroll, swipe between chapters, auto-scroll and its speed. Bionic reading is the one with no native equivalent anywhere in the repo, so it is net-new work rather than a re-plumb.
- **The in-reader novel sheet gets string resources** (owner, 2026-09-03). Every label in `NovelReaderSettingsSheet` except the orientation chips and the colour-filter blend modes is a hardcoded English literal today, so the novel in-reader sheet is untranslated. It is done as part of the sheet collapse rather than as a separate pass.
- **Position is a sealed type answered by the provider, and it names its chapter.** Manga answers a page index and a count; novels answer a fraction, which is what `novel_chapters.last_text_progress` already stores as hundredths. The chapter component is not optional detail: because the viewer holds more than one chapter at a time for seamless scrolling, a position that says only "62%" cannot say 62% of what, and the host's existing `onPageSelected(page)` already carries `page.chapter` for exactly this reason. The navigator and indicator read a derived fraction and label scoped to the visible chapter.
- **The novel provider is proven with a deliberately plain viewer before the tsundoku renderer is lifted**, so a host-plumbing failure and a rendering failure cannot arrive together. **The plain viewer's container shape is settled up front, and it was measured rather than assumed.** It is a `RecyclerView` of chapters whose item view is the renderer's chunk stack. That is the one shape that gets both halves right, and an earlier draft of this doc got it wrong in both directions. Tsundoku uses a scroll view over a linear layout of appended chapters, which is exactly why they need a compensating scroll when they prune: nothing recycles there, so every loaded chapter stays laid out. A recycler lays out only the visible chapter, so a chapter inserted above the reader costs nothing and moves nothing. Their `ChapterTextBlock` is already a linear layout of chunk views, so it drops into a recycler item unchanged and the renderer port pays nothing for this. Verified in `RecyclerPrependPositionTest`; the drift that does exist is described under "Known defects". The plain viewer is that container from the start, holding plain text with no spans, no images and one chapter, and every later step adds into it rather than replacing it.
- **Our TTS transport is kept and tsundoku's text handling is taken.** Measured rather than assumed: we have a `MediaSessionCompat` with lock-screen and headset transport, which tsundoku only gained later in its `TtsPlaybackService` (`4362f0279`) with nothing ours lacks, and tsundoku has an utterance splitter capped at the engine's maximum input length with a no-spaces branch for CJK, which we lack entirely. Neither side requests audio focus, ducks, or has a sleep timer, and neither highlights below paragraph granularity, so the roadmap's in-text-highlight item survives this migration rather than being delivered by it.
- **Seamless chapter-to-chapter scrolling comes from Mihon's mechanism, not tsundoku's.** The host already spans chapters: `WebtoonAdapter.setChapters` builds one item list from the previous, current and next chapters' pages with `ChapterTransition` items between them, `WebtoonViewer` calls `requestPreloadChapter` as a boundary approaches, and `onPageSelected` reports which chapter is now visible through `ReaderPage.chapter`. A text viewer rendering `ViewerChapters` the same way inherits all of it, so novels get the capability from the engine rather than from a second implementation. **Tsundoku's own infinite-scroll machinery is deliberately not ported:** it lives inside their host-coupled `NovelViewer` (a `loadedChapters` list, their own append path, threshold auto-load, cross-chapter progress math, and four `prepare*ChapterForInfiniteScroll` methods on their forked `ReaderViewModel`), it is gated off by default there, and porting it would rebuild what the host already provides while dragging in the fork this takeover exists to avoid. **The decline was drawn one file too wide, and is narrowed here.** `ChapterQueue` (their unbounded append and prepend list with trimming) is genuinely replaced by Mihon's three-chapter `ViewerChapters` and stays declined. `NovelProgress` is not: it is 48 lines of renderer-agnostic arithmetic in `shared/` with no host dependency, and it holds rules the position kernel and the seam step both need anyway. `forwardChaptersToMarkRead(oldIndex, newIndex, size)` is the "a fling scrolled past whole chapters, mark them read" rule, `NEXT_LOAD_RETRY_COOLDOWN_MS` (15s) stops a chapter that keeps failing at the boundary from respawning a request every scroll frame, and `progressToPercent` rounds rather than truncates so a stored position matches between renderers. Its own KDoc, "the slider now seeks only within the current chapter", independently corroborates the chapter-scoped position ruling above. Take it.
- **The settings restructure ships first, before the reader work starts.** It is independent of the migration, it is the visible answer to `unseensnick/Reikai#55`, and it is the one piece a user notices immediately.

- **The two viewers are rebuilt against our architecture; only the layers beneath them are ported** (owner, 2026-09-03, on the condition that a port must integrate rather than bolt on). A third research round measured the seam precisely and it falls below the viewers, not through them. Their forward-only chapter queue is referenced 76 times in the native viewer and 39 in the WebView one, and **zero times in any portable layer**, so it is the viewers' spine rather than a detail. Keeping it would mean running a second chapter-window model beside Mihon's, which is the derive-or-do-not-own defect the program exists to stop; removing it means editing the viewers' core loop at 115 sites, which is a rebuild wearing a port's clothes. Their viewers also consume a text field on `ReaderPage`, which needs the same field on `source-api`'s `Page`, the contract installed extensions compile against. Ruling backward scrolling in (below) settles it, because their model cannot express it at all.
- **Seamless scrolling goes both ways, matching the webtoon viewer** (owner, 2026-09-03). Tsundoku is forward-only and the backward half of their code is dead, so this half has no reference implementation and is net-new work. See the prepend trap under "Known defects" for why the webtoon viewer's own mechanism does not transfer either.
- **The engine's chapter window is its own type, not `ViewerChapters`.** `NovelSource.parseChapter` returns a raw HTML body with no page concept anywhere in the novel source contract, and a downloaded chapter is a single self-contained file with its images inlined. So a novel chapter cannot travel as a `ReaderPage`, and the earlier wording "renders `ViewerChapters` across previous, current and next" is not literally achievable. The engine builds its own three-slot window the way `WebtoonAdapter` builds its item list, over novel chapters as HTML, and Mihon's `ViewerChapters` stays the manga adapter's own type. This is decided at the engine step, not discovered at the seam step.
- **The font manager ships, both halves** (owner, 2026-09-03). Import and in-app download from Google Fonts, ported from tsundoku with three fixes, because import alone would send the user out to a browser and back for every font. Reject woff and woff2 at download time: their validator accepts five formats while their lister shows only TrueType and OpenType, and Android cannot build a typeface from woff2 at all, so a download can currently land as a file the app then hides. Resolve the manager from the graph rather than constructing a second one in the settings screen, which today gives two typeface caches. Keep their curated 29-family list, which includes four CJK families that matter here, so no Google Fonts API key is needed. Storage needs one new directory beside downloads and novel downloads under the user's existing tree, and no new permission.
- **The TTS splitter is built on `BreakIterator`, not ported** (owner, 2026-09-03). Tsundoku's is ASCII-only in its sentence set so CJK terminators never match, hard-cuts mid-word every 4000 characters when the text has no spaces, decides that from a single boolean over the whole text so one Latin sentence flips the strategy for a Japanese chapter, and has a fallback that can emit a chunk longer than the cap. `BreakIterator` is in neither repository today; its sentence instance is locale-correct and its word and line instances use dictionary segmentation for Chinese, Japanese, Thai and Khmer, which is the case tsundoku hard-cuts.
- **Chapter translation and EPUB export are parked** (owner, 2026-09-03), and are not part of this takeover. Translation is roughly 2400 lines across three modules with twelve engines, off by default, and hard-wired to their forked view model. EPUB export is not reachable from the reader at all in their app. Both go to the roadmap's parked section with a revive trigger rather than into the sequence.

### What this must not become

**Option A was built here and reverted.** A single Compose shell hosting both mediums, with the image viewers decoupled from `ReaderActivity` behind a `ViewerHost` interface and embedded through `AndroidView`, was built across two commits and reverted. The record in [unified-reader.md](unified-reader.md) is explicit that the revert was cost, not feasibility: an on-device spike confirmed the webtoon view scrolled and zoomed correctly inside `AndroidView`. The cost was that it diverged the hot image-viewer files from upstream, creating a permanent hand-port tax, and ran modified viewers even on the fallback path.

That decline does not transfer to this takeover, and the reason is precise enough to be a test: Option A's tax came from decoupling the **viewers**, and this takeover neutralizes the **orchestration** above them while leaving the viewers untouched. That is the same line every previous amendment drew. It also means the decline has not expired so much as been routed around, and the routing only holds while the viewers-are-never-modified rule holds. The moment a step edits a viewer, this surface has become Option A and the revert's reasoning applies again in full.

### Known defects and traps in the surfaces being replaced

Recorded so the takeover fixes them deliberately rather than carrying them across, each naming the step that owns it.

- **Five places assume an integer page index** (step 2). The chapter navigator builds its slider as `steps = totalPages - 2` over `valueRange = 1f..totalPages` in `ChapterNavigator`, so a chapter modelled as one stub page produces negative steps. This does not throw, and the earlier wording overstated it: M3's `SliderState` class carries no `require`, the check lives in the `Slider` composable itself, and the navigator composes that only behind a `totalPages > 1` guard, which the vertical path repeats as `showSlider`. The real defect is therefore a silent capability loss, a one-stub-page chapter gets no seekbar at all, and step 2 owes novels a real scrubbing range rather than a crash fix. `moveToPageIndex` resolves a page by list index. `updateChapterProgress` persists `lastPageRead` as an index and fires completion only when `pages.lastIndex == pageIndex`, so a fraction-driven viewer would never mark a chapter read. Download-ahead uses the ratio `page.number / pages.size`. Restoring after process death assigns `requestedPage` an index.
- **A paragraph past the engine's maximum input length went by in silence when read aloud** (step 8). **Resolved before step 5** (`18f3c1543`): `SystemTtsEngine.speak` now splits through `TtsUtteranceSplitter`, built on `BreakIterator` rather than on tsundoku's splitter per the ruling above, and checks each `speak` return value. Step 8's entry has the measurement.
- **`MainActivity` implemented the reader's `NovelVolumeKeyHost`** (step 9, and it could not be earlier). **Resolved in step 9:** the interface went with the legacy screen and `MainActivity`'s class header is back to upstream's shape. It was a compile-level dependency of a Mihon file on the novel reader package. `MainActivity` implemented the interface because the legacy novel reader was a Voyager screen hosted there, and `NovelReaderScreen` registered the handler while it was on screen, so the interface could only be removed once that screen was deleted. Routing volume keys through the viewer's own `handleKeyEvent` was a separate, earlier job at step 4: `WebtoonViewer` already does exactly that, reading a user-settable `volumeScrollDistance` inside `handleKeyEvent` under a `// RK` island marked as novel parity, so the mechanism exists and only needs a second caller.
- **The TTS transport had exactly one owner, and it was being deleted** (step 8). **Resolved in steps 8 and 9:** `ReadAloudController` drives `NovelTtsSession` through `NovelTtsSessionTransport`, and the old `NovelTtsController` and `NovelReaderScreenModel` are gone.
- **The reader settings suffix carried search breadcrumbs** (resolved in step 1, kept here as the record). `contentTypedCategory` existed so a duplicated setting read distinctly and its settings-search breadcrumb differed per content type, which made retiring it a search regression risk rather than a cosmetic change. Step 1 took that risk and cleared it: the helper still has twelve call sites elsewhere in settings, but neither reader screen uses it any more. Nothing outstanding.
- **Nothing on our side of this surface is tested** (every step). No test class touches the novel reader, nothing under any reader path has a test at all, and the repo has no Compose UI tests, so every check below is a device check or a new unit test written against a kernel that did not previously exist. **The imported half is different and that is a reason to import it:** roughly 2400 lines of unit tests sit over exactly the layers being ported, and zero over the two viewers being left behind. Those tests come across with the code.
- **The novel viewers have no dispatch point, and tsundoku's answer is wrong for us** (step 4). `ReadingMode.toViewer` is keyed off `ReadingMode`, which has no novel member, so nothing currently constructs a text viewer. Tsundoku added a `NOVEL` member to the enum and branches inside `toViewer`. Copying that leaks a "Novel" option into the manga reading-mode UI at five call sites (both dropdowns on the manga settings screen, the in-reader reading-mode dialog, the general settings page and the reading-mode page), and one of those slices the list positionally with `entries.drop(1)`, so inserting anywhere but the end silently changes what is dropped. The engine dispatches on content type before it reaches `toViewer` instead.
- **A chapter picked from the chapter list never reached the screen** (found and fixed while taking the surface over). `WebGpuViewer.setChapters` read `currentPage ?: getPage(requestedPage)`, and `currentPage` is null only on the very first call, so every later one discarded the incoming chapter's requested page. The model, the app bar and the page count moved to the new chapter while the viewer kept rendering the old one, which reads as the tap doing nothing. Measured on device before and after: the jump showed 30 pages and saved a Chapter 5 page, and a viewer rebuild alone turned that into 43 pages and a Chapter 2 page. The fix keeps the anchor only when the page on screen belongs to the newly current chapter, which is what separates a jump from scrolling across a boundary: `loadNewChapter` reaches the same method during a seamless crossing, and there the page on screen already belongs to the new chapter, so it still wins and the reader does not lurch. Verified both ways, including a backward crossing landing at the previous chapter's last page rather than its first. **The same line exists verbatim in `refs/mihon`, but the reader's chapter list is Reikai's, so upstream may have no route to it.** The next-chapter button reaches the same call and was measured working before the fix, so what separates the two paths is not established; do not assume the button was ever broken.
- **Menu visibility is host state that lives in the manga provider, and the image viewers are why it cannot simply move** (step 4 or 5, needs a ruling before it does). The amendment assigns menu visibility to the host, but it sits in `ReaderViewModel.State` today and the three image viewers read `state.value.menuVisible` directly at ten sites to gate long-tap and volume keys (three in `PagerViewer`, four in `WebtoonViewer`, one of which hides the menu on a scroll, and three in `WebGpuViewer`). A field read cannot be intercepted by a forwarder, so relocating the state means editing all three, which adds a mechanism past the ones content-layer.md's Reader row enumerates. Nothing breaks in the meantime, because the image viewers only run in a manga session. The writes are not the problem: `ReaderActivity`'s `toggleMenu`, `showMenu` and `hideMenu` are already Activity methods the viewers call, so they absorb into `ReaderHost` for free. This is the one place where "the host owns menu visibility" and "the image viewers are never diverged further" cannot both hold, and the step that moves it owes the owner that choice rather than making it in passing.
- **`EntryId` has to cross an Intent boundary** (step 4). `ReaderActivity.newIntent` takes a nullable `mangaId` and puts it in a `"manga"` extra alongside `"chapter"`, `"page_index"` and Reikai's `"source_scoped"`. Identity in the shared layer is the sealed `EntryId`, which an Intent extra cannot hold directly, so how it is encoded and decoded is a step 4 decision rather than something to improvise at the call site.
- **Two novel-reader settings were only editable on the manga screen** (step 11). The novel reader read `ReaderPreferences.verticalNavigatorHeight` and `verticalNavigatorOnLeft` for its progress rail, but both rows lived on the manga reader screen. **Resolved in the parity block below:** every novel reader reads `NovelPreferences.readerRailHeight` and `readerRailOnLeft`, whose rows are on the novel reader screen, so step 11 owes this nothing.
- **Tsundoku ships four dead or unwired novel preferences** (step 6, as a do-not-port list). `novelKeepChaptersLoaded` and `novelGlobalPresets` have only their declarations, `novelCustomCss` and `novelCustomJs` are read but have no writer since the snippet lists superseded them, and `novelTapToScroll` has a settings row that no viewer reads. The "Theme null" row this bullet used to name as a fourth item was already fixed upstream when the bullet was written, in tsundoku `c4e87b492`, so it is struck rather than carried.
- **Two of tsundoku's preference reactions rebuild the whole document** (step 6). Their observer routes paragraph indent, paragraph spacing and text-selectable through a full `reloadContent`, which clears every view and re-fetches, re-preprocesses and re-parses every loaded chapter. Indent and spacing are applied purely as spans over an already-built `SpannableStringBuilder` and nothing in the parse depends on them; text-selectable only switches the `PrecomputedText` path plus three view properties. This is the same class as Reikai's known `generalSettings` DOM-rebuild trap, so the wiring is not inherited as-is. Related and worth checking on device rather than assuming: their font size sits in the live-refresh bucket, but the renderer bakes it into the spacing and indent pixel values, so those spans look like they stay stale until the next reload.

- **Drift is a within-chapter problem, not a between-chapter one** (steps 5 and 6). This was measured rather than reasoned, in `RecyclerPrependPositionTest`, after an earlier draft of this doc asserted the opposite and was wrong. Inserting a chapter above the reader moves nothing, even when that chapter then grows by five and a half screens, because a recycler never lays out an item that is entirely above the viewport and the layout manager anchors from the visible child. **That holds only while the screen is full**, which a short chapter with nothing below it is not; the correction and what it changed are under the WebView inventory's rulings. What does move the reader is a chunk **inside the visible chapter, above the reading position**, finishing its measurement: the displacement is exactly the growth. Growth below the reading position is invisible. So compensation belongs inside the chapter view when a chunk re-measures, which is what an asynchronous precomputed-text application, a font-size change or a picture landing causes, and the seamless step needs none of it. All three are handled since: text joins the list only once its height is final, and a restyle and the image re-measure both run inside `holdingReader`. The measurements are in the test's logcat output under tag `PrependSpike`, and each case asserts the drift it measured.
- **Backward preload has one narrow trigger** (step 5). The webtoon viewer preloads backward only when an upward scroll leaves a `Prev` transition item first-visible, and in the steady seamless case no transition item exists at all. There is no page-count-based backward heuristic to copy, so a text viewer needs its own trigger.
- **A single-page chapter marks itself read on entry** (step 4, and the reason novels must not travel as pages). Completion is `pages.lastIndex == pageIndex`, so with one page it is `0 == 0` immediately: the chapter is marked read, trackers sync and it can be deleted the moment the reader opens. Download-ahead also fires unconditionally because the ratio is `1.0` against a `0.25` threshold, and `requestedPage` can only ever be zero, so intra-chapter restore is structurally impossible. Tsundoku hit this and patched around it, with a comment in their own view model saying that for novel chapters marking complete via page index would always fire on selection.
- **`startPostponedEnterTransition` has exactly one call site in the whole reader package** (step 4), inside `updateViewer`, and the trap it looks like is dormant: nothing in this tree or upstream calls `postponeEnterTransition`, so a path that misses it starts nothing rather than leaving a blank reader. Recorded because the call reads as load-bearing (its comment names a crash on API 26) and a future `postponeEnterTransition` would arm it.
- **One saved-state key does two jobs** (step 2). `page_index` is both the launch extra Reikai added for the gallery page-preview jump and the key the view model reads and writes for process-death restore. A fractional position breaks both at once and breaks them silently, because a wrong-typed read returns the default and falls through to the stored page. **Resolved in step 2:** the launch jump moved to its own `launch_page` key and both sources are now consumed once, by the chapter they were meant for.
- **The restore index leaks between chapters** (step 2). `requestedPage` is re-applied to every chapter that becomes current rather than only the first, so a position from the previous chapter carries into the next and is saved only by the viewers' clamp against the last index. **Resolved in step 2**, and it was broader than recorded here: the index is rewritten on every progress save, so after the first page turn of a session every chapter opened afterwards inherited the previous one.
- **The host is coupled to the manga model as a concrete type at roughly 90 sites** (step 3), and its state, dialog and event types appear in composable signatures. Measured 2026-09-04, correcting an earlier "41 places" that matches no count that can be reproduced: `viewModel.` appears 59 times in `ReaderActivity` plus 13 method references, with 33 lines reading its state. Seven viewer files reach the model through the Activity at 17 sites, not four files at six, and the viewers touch 14 distinct Activity members in all. Two ordering hazards sit exactly where the takeover cuts, and there are three rather than two: a documented one where custom info and the merge group must resolve before the state update that builds the viewer, and two undocumented ones, that `manga -> updateViewer` must register before `viewerChapters -> setChapters` because the latter reads the viewer the former installs, and that the view model's own collector must register before both, which holds only because the Activity touches the model before registering its own.
- **A chapter cannot be one TextView** (step 6). Tsundoku splits into views of at least 6000 characters, cutting after the next newline so a chunk never ends mid-paragraph, and their class documentation gives the reason: with one view, layout, span lookup and selection hit-testing are all order-of-chapter, and large chapters caused constant garbage collection and multi-second touch handling. **This is also where the reader can actually be displaced.** A chunk finishing its measurement above the reading position moves the reader by exactly its growth, measured in `RecyclerPrependPositionTest`, so applying precomputed text or a style change to an already-visible chapter owes a compensating scroll. Growth below the reading position is free. Other costs to inherit knowingly: a selection cannot span a chunk boundary, an image span's owning view is found by scanning every chunk, and precomputed text is used only when selection is off, each application guarded against a metrics-mismatch throw.
- **Our TTS was predicted to stall on an oversized paragraph, and it skipped one instead** (step 8). **Resolved before step 5**, with the bullet above: the Fold's engine accepted the oversized utterance and failed it later through `onError`, so the reader advanced past a paragraph it never spoke. A synchronous `ERROR` return, the stall this bullet predicted, is still guarded: `SystemTtsEngine.speak` aborts on one, which completes the pending callback instead of leaving it set.

### Settings the takeover owes

**Why this inventory is one-sided, stated rather than implied** (owner, 2026-09-05). Write-once says cost and differing structure are not exits and only a named mechanism is, so the mechanism is named here once: the typographic settings below (font size and family, line height, margins, alignment, indent, paragraph spacing, reader themes, custom fonts, regex replacements) act on text, and a manga page is an image the source ships, so there is no text for them to act on. That is a property of the content, not of the engines, and it is what makes them novel-only rather than novel-first. Anything in this section that is **not** text-shaped does not get that exemption and is scoped by type like any other change.

The plan originally had no settings dimension, which is where the user-visible regression sits: the app-level screens were restructured in step 1, but nothing said what happens to the settings whose *implementation* is inside the tree being deleted. This section is that inventory. Every row is carried or dropped, nothing is deferred to another item, and an unmarked row means the takeover is not finished.

**Implementation and only UI both gone (carried, at the renderer step).** Font size, line height, side padding, text alignment, font family, background colour and text colour reach the page only as `--readerSettings-*` CSS custom properties written by `NovelReaderHtmlBuilder` and rewritten live by `core.js`, and they are surfaced only in the in-reader sheet. Deleting the tree removes the implementation and the sole UI in one commit while the preference keys survive in the store, so a user would lose font choice and reader themes outright with nothing left on the app-level screen to hint they existed. The follow-system-theme toggle rides along, since it only resolves into the last two. Bundled fonts load through a `FontFace` from `file:///android_asset/fonts/`; a native renderer loads them with `FontFamily(Font(path, assetManager))`, which the current sheet already does for its own chip previews. Note that padding is a single value today and becomes tsundoku's four separate margins.

**Row survives, implementation dies (carried, reimplemented).** Bionic reading, remove extra spacing, tap edges to scroll (the tap layout since step 11), swipe between chapters, auto-scroll and auto-scroll speed all keep their rows on the app-level novel screen and keep toggling after the deletion with nothing behind them. Bionic reading is the loudest, because it is an accessibility row, a sheet toggle and a selectable bottom-bar button at once, and `textVide` has no native equivalent anywhere in the repo, so it is net-new work rather than a re-plumb.

**Implementation and only UI both gone, read-aloud (carried, at the TTS step).** Every read-aloud row lives only in the dying sheet's TTS tab, not on the app-level screen: enable, engine, languages, voice, speed, pitch, auto page advance and scroll to top. Step 8 carries all of them except enable onto the app-level novel screen, adds keep-in-view and the highlight style, colour and text colour (owner, 2026-09-13), and retires enable in the new host, since the bottom-bar picker now decides whether the read-aloud button exists. Step 9 deleted the old reader, the last thing reading enable.

**Infrastructure every setting above sits on (carried, at the plumbing step).** These are not settings but nothing works without them, and all four are WebView-only today. The injected scroll listener plus `core.js` reports progress, and that one channel drives the percentage overlay, the seekbar, resume position and the 97% auto-mark-read. The centre-tap chrome toggle is a `core.js` message. The scroll handles that volume-key paging and rail scrubbing call into are `evaluateJavascript`. And TTS paragraph enumeration is entirely `core.js`'s `tts` object driven by five one-line JS calls, so read-aloud does not work at all until a renderer owns paragraph enumeration, which is why the TTS step stays after the renderer step.

**Newly possible, carried (owner, 2026-09-03).** Paragraph indent and paragraph spacing, which have no preference today and are hardcoded margins in the vendored CSS. User regex find-and-replace rules. Custom fonts through a font manager. And the rendering-mode picker itself, which is a new setting rather than a replacement for one. The first four were previously held by the roadmap's tsundoku feature-harvest line, which no longer exists as a separate item.

**Survives untouched.** Orientation, both the global default and the per-novel `viewer_flags` override. Keep screen on. The brightness and colour-filter overlay itself, a native shared `ReaderContentOverlay` outside the tree; a novel session reads its own keys for it since step 11. Skip duplicate chapters, mark read on skip, bottom-bar button selection, incognito behaviour, and download-ahead.

**Dropped.** The two floating-TTS-puck position keys become orphans when the puck is deleted, and they are state rather than options, so nothing replaces them.

**Carried, but last (owner, 2026-09-03; narrowed 2026-09-14).** Of tsundoku's extras, only the ordered in-reader bottom bar is still owed, as step 10 below. The font manager and auto-split landed at step 6, `novelMarkShortChapterAsRead` was declined for the leave rule, the CSS and JS snippet editors, parked on 2026-09-10, shipped at step 11, and the reading status bar, in-reader text edit mode and quotes were parked on 2026-09-14 as maybe later. None of this was required for parity, so it lands after the cutover, and before the sheet collapse, because collapsing the sheets around a settings set that is about to grow would mean laying it out twice.

**Parked, not part of this takeover (owner, 2026-09-03).** Chapter translation and EPUB export. Translation is roughly 2400 lines across three modules, twelve engines, off by default and wired into their forked view model; EPUB export is not reachable from their reader at all. Both keep a roadmap line with a revive trigger.

**Parked (owner, 2026-09-10), then shipped at step 11 (owner, 2026-09-14): user-authored CSS and JS snippets.** The record of the park: Tsundoku's `NovelWebViewStyler` reads a `CodeSnippet` model, titled and individually enablable, and reapplies the JS half on append. Reikai has no snippet feature at all, so step 7 takes the styler's CSS assembly and leaves that path behind rather than importing a settings surface nobody asked for. Revive trigger: someone wanting per-source styling that the source CSS priority toggle cannot express.

**The parity direction, for the record.** Tsundoku's in-reader novel page is 2010 lines against our 583, and their app-level novel screen 445 against our 140, across five in-reader tabs to our three. The takeover is not chasing that number for its own sake. The bar it is held to is that nothing a Reikai user has today disappears; the newly-possible group and the extras group are what close most of the remaining gap, and what stays unported is recorded in the do-not-port note above rather than left implicit.

### The host's ordering hazards

Four traps sit exactly where the takeover cuts, each now carrying an `// RK` comment where it sits. They are the reason navigation moved at step 4 rather than step 3 (owner, 2026-09-04).

1. `ReaderViewModel`'s own `init` collector stamps `currentChapter.requestedPage`, and the viewers read that field the moment chapters arrive in their `setChapters`; it wins only because it subscribes during model construction, forced by the Activity's first dereference of `viewModel`.
2. `ReaderActivity.setChapters` removes the spinner unconditionally and then delivers to the viewer, so a mis-order is a permanently black reader with no spinner and no error. It works only because `onCreate` registers the `manga` collector ahead of the `viewerChapters` one, and beneath that because `ReaderViewModel.init` sets `manga` before `viewerChapters` in one sequential body. Nothing enforces either half.
3. `setMangaReadingMode` re-emits the same `ViewerChapters` instance, which `distinctUntilChanged` swallows in front of both collectors, leaving `Event.ReloadViewerChapters` as the only delivery channel.
4. The event channel was RENDEZVOUS with `trySend`, so an event was dropped whenever no receiver was parked; upstream still has that shape. It is `Channel.UNLIMITED` behind an `// RK` island that records why a lost `PageChanged` does not heal (ruled fixed before the engine moved any event, owner, 2026-09-04).

### The typed position (step 2)

The kernel carries the completion predicate as well as the position, because completion used to fire only when the page index equalled the chapter's last index, which a fraction can never satisfy. Two defects the takeover would otherwise have carried across were fixed here: the chrome tore at a chapter boundary (the page number came from the page's own chapter while the total and title came from the active one), and the restore index leaked between chapters, armed by every page turn rather than only by a gallery jump, since the index is rewritten on each progress save. The three image viewers, `ReaderChapter.requestedPage` and every interactor were left untouched. Device-verified on the emulator across both navigators; a one-page chapter's navigator was not, since none was to hand, and its arithmetic is pinned by unit tests instead.

Three things the step turned up that the plan did not predict: the sealed unit already existed as the recents rows' `RecentsProgress`, so it was moved and renamed rather than written; the kernel had to be typed on primitives, because `ReaderPage` reaches `android.net.Uri` through `Page` and the repo carries no Robolectric; and grepping the tree for an existing neutral type, plus checking what a kernel's parameter types drag in, is the lesson the later steps inherited.

### The engine and the viewer contract (step 3)

**The host resolves the engine through `viewModels { }` over `graph.viewModelFactory.createManuallyAssistedFactory(...)`**, wrapped in `viewModelFactory { initializer { } }`, because that call returns a `() -> FactoryType` rather than a `ViewModelProvider.Factory`. `ReaderActivity` is the production precedent for the manual factory; the Compose route, `assistedMetroViewModel`, is not reachable from an Activity.

**The binding constraint** is that the three image viewers are constructed with the concrete `ReaderActivity` (`ReadingMode.toViewer`) and call back into it directly at roughly forty sites, which no wrapping adapter can intercept. So the engine owns the downward viewer contract only; the Activity stays the object viewers talk to and forwards into the engine, with its upward methods (`onPageSelected`, `requestPreloadChapter`, the menu toggles) as thin forwarders.

**The downward surface is exactly six members** of `Viewer`: `getView`, `destroy`, `setChapters`, `moveToPage`, `handleKeyEvent` and `handleGenericMotionEvent`. Two of them, `setChapters` and `moveToPage`, are manga-typed through `ViewerChapters` and `ReaderPage`, so they stay on the manga adapter; the other four are already neutral. **Viewer-shape questions were asked by six smart casts**, one for the navigator's reading direction in `ReaderActivity` and five in the settings sheet, covering continuity, verticality, the webtoon branch and dual-page mode. They are the capability the contract owes, and absorbing one and leaving five would have left the sheet resolving shape by instance check while the host no longer could. The five now resolve through one helper, `Viewer?.running()` in `MangaReaderSettingsPages`, which each page reads instead of casting.

**Menu visibility stays with the host**, which is what the amendment's hard line says: `setMenuVisibility` is half `WindowInsetsControllerCompat` and is replayed from `onResume` and `onWindowFocusChanged`, so the state cannot leave the host without the insets call following it. It still lives in `ReaderViewModel.State` and the three image viewers read it directly at ten sites, which is the one place where "the host owns menu visibility" and "the image viewers are never diverged further" cannot both hold. Nothing breaks meanwhile, because the image viewers only run in a manga session.

**Step 3's inventory.** Present: the dialog slot with all six cases raised and dismissed, the host raising `Loading` off `isLoadingAdjacentChapter`, the three page actions each run to its effect, the chapter list's contents and all four row verbs, the viewport install and teardown, reading direction flipping the navigator, and the settings sheet resolving shape off the live viewer. Key and motion routing is present with its delegation unit-tested but not exercised on device, because volume-key navigation is off by default. Deliberately dropped: nothing. Missing: nothing, though a later round found three manga behaviours that changed rather than vanished.

**Three manga behaviours changed rather than vanished, and two are kept on purpose.** `onNewIntent` is net-new, so a second launch while the reader is open now restarts on a different entry or chapter instead of being dropped, which is why a chapter notification works (kept, owner, 2026-09-06). The app bar's WebView, browser and share actions gate on the resolved chapter URL rather than on `state.source is HttpSource`, so they appear when the URL resolves and stay hidden if a source throws, which is this program's capability-slot rule rather than upstream's early-and-dead control (kept). And `getChapterUrl` was running on the main thread through the provider's `webUrl`, where upstream keeps it in `launchIO`; that one was a defect, fixed with `flowOn(Dispatchers.IO)`.

**Entry points walked:** the library's continue-reading button, a details chapter row, the details Resume button, a History row, and an Updates row on a different source. ### What the audits and review rounds found

**A mid-step audit (2026-09-06) found one silent manga regression, and it is the shape to watch for.** The chapter sheet stopped opening scrolled to the chapter being read: when its rows became a flow, `rememberLazyListState` captured the position on the first composition, before the rows arrived. A device pass had already "verified" it on a six-chapter series, where row zero and the current chapter look the same, so the check could not fail. Also fixed in that round: an optimistic delete-swipe override outranking a live queue state, the sheet rebuilding its whole list on the main thread twice a second while downloading, teardown building a reader session for a launch that named no entry, a collector started inside a composition, and a prefetch swallowing cancellation. **The lesson is to pick the device case that can fail**, not the one that is convenient.

**A review round over the whole unpushed range found four defects the gates did not**, all of them the host reading manga's model for something a novel launch never fills, or the pipeline's contract being dropped in our wiring: a `.txt` chapter reached the WebView unescaped and unsanitised, the bookmark control rendered permanently empty, a chapter that failed to load left the previous one on screen in silence, and the reading percentage never appeared while its setting sat on the novel screen doing nothing. Each is now the session's own answer through `ReaderProvider`, which gained `loadState`, `retryLoad`, `bookmarked`, `toggleBookmark`, `webUrl` and `showProgress`.

**Steps 0 to 6 were audited as one range** (`0f751005d..f2251cb2e`, 97 commits over 145 files). Two defects were proved on the emulator: a programmatic scroll never settled, so an auto-scrolled read persisted nothing (`RecyclerView.scrollBy` does not change scroll state, and `onProgressSettled` was reached only from an idle transition), and auto-scroll kept running while the reader was not visible. Four more were code-grounded: inline images sized to the display width rather than the text column, pipeline output cached per chapter with no invalidation, a failed chapter switch leaving the bookmark writing to the wrong chapter, and font mirroring doing a SAF lookup plus a whole-file copy on the main thread from view construction. Two settings were shown enabled and did nothing in the native mode, which the content-layer rule says must be hidden rather than left as silent no-ops. The 97% completion rule was the one pin-once failure, and `SettingsReaderScreen.kt` left the tree as a rename rather than a delete, so the `pre-commit` manifest guard missed it (it read `--diff-filter=D`, which does not match `R`).

**Everything in that audit is fixed**, each verified the way its defect was found: progress persists from the reported position, debounced and flushed on pause (the same 20 second run that wrote no rows writes 39% now); auto-scroll stops off screen and a scrub stops it; an image fills the text column; the session's chapter advances only once a chapter has rendered; the loader says when a chapter-text setting changes and both readers rebuild; the two embedded-content rows are hidden in native; both readers ask the completion kernel; the font mirror resolves in a coroutine, caches its misses and refreshes when the file changes; and the renamed settings screen has a manifest row, with the guard now reading a rename's source path.

**The security posture is inherited, not introduced, and the hardening was pulled forward** (owner, 2026-09-08) because the sanitiser is in the shared pipeline every mode runs, including the legacy reader that was still the default. Three bypasses stood when the review ran (`</script >` with a space, comment splicing, and no attribute inspection at all), and four free-form preferences were interpolated unescaped into the document's `<style>` while backup restore writes almost every preference key, which made a shared backup file an injection route. The WebView target now prunes the parsed tree (`NovelHtmlUtils.sanitizeForWebView`): frames, plugin elements, `base`, `meta`, `form`, `svg`, `math`, `noembed` and `noframes` go, `xmp` and `plaintext` become `pre`, every `on*` attribute and `javascript:` URL goes unless "Run scripts a chapter embeds" is on, and comments are cut out of text nodes only. `TEXT_VIEW` keeps its pattern cleanup deliberately, because `Html.fromHtml` has no script engine and reparsing would cost the line breaks a plain-text chapter is rebuilt with. The four preferences are checked against the shape each can legitimately have and replaced by the default when they do not match, rather than escaped, since CSS escaping is per-context. `NovelHtmlSanitizerTest` and `NovelReaderCssValuesTest` pin it, each case seen red before its fix or under mutation. What is left is the posture itself: the WebView needs JavaScript for the renderer and the cookie jar for the sources, and `loadDataWithBaseURL` takes the plugin's own declared site as the document origin.

**The sanitiser's cost was measured and needs no fix.** A 4.8 MB chapter (six base64-inlined images, the shape a downloaded chapter takes) sanitises in 47 ms on the JVM at the default settings, once per chapter, inside `viewModelScope.launchIO`, with the next chapter prefetched ahead of the user. Its `Regex.replace` passes do not each copy the string: `Matcher.replaceAll` returns the input instance when nothing matched, and removing every comment from the fixture moved 47.2 ms to 45.6 ms, so guarding each pass with a `contains` check trades one scan for another. The one wasteful path is the Jsoup round-trip that strips `style` attributes, 59 ms of the 106 ms measured with embedded CSS off, left alone deliberately: guarding it on the attribute name changes the output for malformed markup Jsoup would have repaired.

### Seamless reading (step 5)

**Owner rulings, 2026-09-08.** A failed boundary load takes **both** retry mechanisms, the self-clearing cooldown and the manual-retry latch, because tsundoku has run each alone and each failed its own way: the cooldown alone respawns a request every scroll frame, and the latch alone strands auto-append for the rest of the session once a chapter switch destroys the banner that was its only clearer. The window is **three slots**, matching the webtoon reader, so eviction stays implicit in the rebuild. Seamless is **on by default with a preference to turn it off** (`readerSeamlessChapters`), a deliberate novel-only capability: the manga webtoon viewer is always seamless and gains no twin toggle, because its seam is between image pages a reader already treats as continuous, where a novel chapter boundary is a place some readers want to stop. The forward auto-load trigger is a fixed constant rather than a preference; superseded (owner, 2026-09-17), see Decisions. A seamless crossing is **not a skip**: `markReadOnSkip` stays gated to explicit steps. Of tsundoku's `NovelProgress`, three pieces transfer (`NEXT_LOAD_RETRY_COOLDOWN_MS` at 15 seconds, `progressToPercent`'s rounding, and `forwardChaptersToMarkRead`) and `DONE_THRESHOLD` at `0.99f` does not.

**Per-chapter geometry could not come from the recycler.** `NovelTextViewport.percent` read `computeVerticalScrollRange` and `computeVerticalScrollOffset`, which `ScrollbarHelper` estimates from the average laid-out item size once more than one item is laid out: exact at one item, drifting at three. Progress comes from the visible chapter's own item bounds instead (`ChapterScrollProgress`, unit-tested and mutation-tested), which subtracts the viewport for every chapter rather than only the last, unlike tsundoku's `calculateCurrentChapterProgress`.

**What landed.** 5a replaced the scroll metrics; 5b turned the viewport's single-chapter fields into slots, each owning its block, restore position and render state, with `BlockAdapter` dispatching a `DiffUtil` result and `itemAnimator` off, matching `WebtoonAdapter`, which never appends incrementally either. 5c made reading seamless both ways and absorbed 5d: the model publishes a `Window` carrying a generation, an anchor and the chapters, the host drives `ChapterWindow`'s append, prepend and evict verbs, and the viewport reports the visible chapter back so the chrome, progress, mark-as-read and history follow it. Three defects surfaced there: the renderer aborted on `!container.isAttachedToWindow`, and a chapter queued below the reader is never attached until it has a height, so liveness became an explicit `ChapterTextBlock.discarded` flag; a progress report did not name its chapter, so a report arriving mid-crossing landed on the departed chapter's row; and the host's first cut raced two collectors, which the generation counter replaced. The seam marker draws Mihon's own `TransitionText`, made neutral by taking two chapter names, riding above the chapter it introduces so a position still names a chapter.

**Eviction above the reading position was measured rather than assumed** (`RecyclerPrependPositionTest.evictAboveReadingPositionIsFree`, 0 pixels), so nothing compensates for it by hand the way tsundoku's `cleanupDistantChapters` must inside a `NestedScrollView`.

**5e's failure policy.** The real defect was the opposite of a retry storm: a failed warm was swallowed with a log, the rebuild dropped the uncached id, and the seam only draws above a chapter that has a predecessor, so the reader reached the end of a chapter and simply stopped, with no marker, no message and no way to ask again. `NovelWarmPolicy` is a pure predicate over one recorded failure and monotonic uptime, pinned by `NovelWarmPolicyTest` and mutation-tested; a failure suppresses an unprompted warm for fifteen seconds and is dropped by a manual retry or any explicit open. `warmNeighbour` gained the in-flight guard it never had. **The affordance is in the viewport, not the chrome**, because the engine owns no banner and manga's equivalent is already inside its viewer: `NovelBoundaryFailureView` draws at whichever window edge ran out of text, pushed through `ChapterWindow.setBoundaryFailures` in the same emission that carries the chapters, with bound holders updated in place rather than through `notifyItemRangeChanged`, since a rebind re-measures the text of the chapter the reader is inside. The retry draws its own progress, which is why `BoundaryFailure` carries the failure's timestamp: a retry that failed the same way would otherwise be an equal value and the push would be swallowed.

**One asymmetry with manga is left open, and it is the policy rather than the affordance.** `WebtoonTransitionHolder.setError` gives manga the same inline message, Retry button and loading state, so what the reader sees is at parity. Underneath, `ReaderViewModel.preload` readmits a failed chapter on its very next trigger with no cooldown, so novels wait where manga does not. Levelling it either way is an owner call: removing ours contradicts the ruling that put it there, and adding one to the image viewers spends a marker on files the takeover holds to the divergences content-layer.md's Reader row lists.

### The native renderer and its settings (step 6)

**The seam went in first.** `TextViewport` is a capability carrying `load` and `applySettings`, which the host asks for with `as?` rather than casting to a concrete class; the chapter document moved into the viewport, since the HTML is its own format. The two readers' byte-identical `loadChapterHtml` twins became one session-scoped `NovelChapterTextLoader`, and the pipeline is ported into `reikai/novel/content/`: seven stages, the eight preferences on `ln_reader_*` keys, and a Chapter text settings group for the six with a UI. Their markers are renamed from `data-tsundoku-*` to `data-reikai-*`. The one behaviour change is that `sanitizeForRender` strips scripts by default, so a chapter's own scripts no longer run unless the setting says they may. Markdown is still unreachable, since `detectTextKind` picks it only from a `.md` chapter URL no current source produces.

**The renderer went in as a third mode and the styling layer is net-new**, as the scout predicted: `NovelTextStyle` applies size, line spacing, alignment, padding, colour and the bundled faces through `Typeface.createFromAsset`, replacing the `file:///android_asset` font loading that died with `core.js`. Two things the device found that the port did not: progress must not be reported before the text is measured, since a recycler with no scroll range reported 100%, which at the completion threshold marks the chapter read and retires its download; and the renderer takes the HTML built for a WebView sink rather than the pipeline's `NovelChapterContent`, because `LoadedChapter` carries only the escaped string.

**Links and text selection are exclusive, by preference rather than by gap.** Tsundoku wires `LinkOnlyMovementMethod` only when selection is off and `ArrowKeyMovementMethod` when it is on, so a link is dead in their default configuration too. Ours is `ln_reader_text_selectable`, off by default, with a row hidden unless the rendering mode is NATIVE. **The cause of selection not working at all was one attribute**, not the recycler claiming the gesture as an earlier draft assumed: `viewer_container` sets `android:descendantFocusability="blocksDescendants"` (upstream's, mihon `c8f70efde`), and a TextView that cannot take focus never initialises the `Editor` that draws selection handles. `NovelTextViewport` lifts it to `FOCUS_AFTER_DESCENDANTS` on attach and restores it on detach, scoped to this viewport and to selection being on, so the image viewers keep upstream's blocking; tsundoku does the same thing and names the same cause. Mutation-tested by disabling the registration, and device-verified. **What the link branch cost, found only by running it:** the chunk views cover the whole chapter, so a tap on text never reached the item view and the chrome could not be toggled at all; the tap listener moved onto the chunk views, which works because `LinkOnlyMovementMethod` declines a tap that is not on a link.

**Step 6's remaining scope was four times what the handoff carried**, found by sweeping every `NovelReaderSettings` field against what the native path actually reads. That sweep is the reusable part: any field the viewport never reads is either the host's job, gated to a later step, or a silent no-op. It turned up the three display settings with no UI in the shared host (font family, line spacing, alignment), and bionic reading, remove-extra-spacing and volume-key scrolling, all three dead in native because they lived in `core.js`.

**The bionic port is the vendored bundle's own table**, decoded out of the minified asset: `text-vide` bolds a word's length minus the index of the first boundary it fits in, over `[0, 4, 12, 17, 24, 29, 35, 42, 48]`. Pinned by `NovelBionicSpansTest` over `NovelBionicSpans.boldLengthFor`, and cross-checked against the page's own pass by `NovelWebDocumentTest.bionicBoldsTheLettersTheNativeRendererDoes`. Remove-extra-spacing went into the shared pipeline rather than the renderer, so it covers all three modes.

**A claimed defect here was wrong, and the device is what caught it.** `NovelChapterTextLoader` hardcoded `RenderTarget.WEB_VIEW`, which was written up as CSS text rendering visibly in native mode. It does not: `NovelTextRenderer.normalizeHtmlForRendering` strips `style, script` unconditionally before `Html.fromHtml`, as tsundoku's renderer does. Proved with a crafted downloaded chapter carrying a style block, a script block and a plain-text control line. The target is passed correctly now for honesty, not repair. **Follow the data to the screen before calling something a bug.**

**Where tsundoku's font manager should not be copied.** Their download prefers a `.ttf` but falls back to the first URL of any format, accepts woff and woff2 at validation, and then lists only `.ttf`/`.otf`, so the font is invisible forever. It rarely fires only because their User-Agent is a Chrome string with the version token missing, which Google Fonts reads as an old browser: that UA returns 2 TTF URLs where a real modern Chrome UA returns 14 woff2 and no TTF, so anyone "fixing" it breaks their feature silently. Ours rejects any URL that is not TTF or OTF. **A WOFF decoder was considered and declined** (owner, 2026-09-07): Android's font stack takes SFNT only, while WebView takes woff2 fine since it is Chromium, so accepting woff2 would work in one rendering mode and silently not in the other. WOFF1 would be about 150 lines on the JDK's own inflater; WOFF2 needs the glyf/loca inverse transform and a JNI wrapper around Google's C++ library.

**Custom fonts, with three findings.** Our own `Reikai/<version>` agent is answered in TTF, so no browser string is spoofed, while the client default returns only woff2, and the `fonts.google.com/download?family=` endpoint tsundoku builds a URL for now returns HTML. Both renderers read an app-private mirror of the file, because a picked folder is a SAF tree with no path that neither `Typeface.createFromFile` nor the WebView can open. And the web layer is given the font's readable name rather than its file name, because `font-family` with a dot in it is invalid CSS. **The picker is a screen rather than a list dialog** (owner), searching the whole Google Fonts catalogue from the keyless metadata endpoint, with every row but Default drawn in the face it offers. Sans serif, Serif and Monospace are offered and were missing; Georgia, Times New Roman and Arial, which tsundoku lists, are folded by Android's alias table into two faces already present.

**The regex editor's preview and the reader run through one compile kernel** (`NovelRegexReplacements.compile`), which is what stops the two disagreeing. Tsundoku keeps them apart and their editor has three faults because of it: Save silently returns on a blank name or pattern, their Test compiles with no options while the processor defaults to case-insensitive, and their non-regex Test takes the replacement literally while the processor reads a `$` as a group reference. Ours makes a text rule's replacement literal and disables Save until the rule is complete.

**The image getter** drops tsundoku's host coupling entirely: their Activity becomes a `Context`, and the Referer comes from the chapter's own base URL the viewport already holds. Their `tsundoku-novel-image://` branch is not taken, because a downloaded chapter carries its images inline as data URLs. **Sizing needed the precomputed layout rebuilt, not a `requestLayout`**: a `PrecomputedText` caches the measurement it was built from, and a selectable view needs its text set again instead, since its `DynamicLayout` reflows on a text or span edit and on nothing else. **The spacing left over was ours, not the platform's**: line height is a multiplier and a line holding an image is as tall as the image, so a full-width picture gained half its own height in blank space. It is applied as the equivalent number of pixels now, measured from the font once size and typeface are set, so prose keeps its spacing and an image line is left alone. **Since the reader audit:** a picture narrower than the column keeps its own width, as the page's `max-width: 100%` draws it, and one that fails is drawn as a box with Retry when its address can be asked again (`ImageFailureDrawable`, `reader.js`); the retry reads no cache, since the HTTP cache keeps the failed answer. **The line at the top is held across a picture landing** (2026-09-15), which is how the native renderer stopped moving the reader when images above them arrived: the re-measure sets every dirty chunk inside one `holdingReader`, as a restyle does. Reserving the real box up front was weighed and declined, since the size needs a header request per image and it could not cover a failed picture collapsing, and making the chunk the recycler item was a re-plumb of everything that indexes chunks. A landing still waiting on the pictures is left to land instead. Pinned by `theLineAtTheTopStaysThereWhenPicturesAboveItArrive` over both renderers.

**Margins, indent and spacing are mostly at tsundoku's numbers**: indent and spacing are multiples of the text size, the margins are dp at their defaults of 16 on three edges and 50 on top, and their ranges of 0 to 64 dp and 0 to 5em are taken. Our single padding is retired, carried into the left and right margins, the edges it padded in every shipped build, by a version-gated migration, which is why `versionCode` moved to 191. **Paragraph spacing is deliberately not theirs** (owner, after a side-by-side on the Fold against tsundoku 0.3.3 with every value matched): it defaults to 1.5em against their 0.5 and its ceiling is 4em against their 3, because the number means different things in the two apps. Measured on the same chapter at the same settings, their WebView drew 27px of extra gap and their TextView 167px, while both of ours drew 25px, because their renderer keeps the blank line `Html.fromHtml` inserts and adds the setting on top of it.

**Three defects came out of that device pass, and the first two are why the settings appeared not to respond.** Restyling flattened the chapter with `toString()`, destroying every span, so any settings change stripped the emphasis, links, images and the paragraph spans themselves; it copies into a `SpannableStringBuilder` now. The WebView and legacy readers could not answer a live change at all, because the only live channel was the vendored push, which carried one padding value; the four margins became CSS variables and both WebView hosts rewrite all six on every settings change. Third, the same number drew a different gap in each renderer, because `Html.fromHtml` separates blocks with a blank line that sat underneath the spacing span; that line is collapsed now. **Two deliberate divergences from tsundoku:** they keep that blank line, and they set all four margins as padding on every chunk view, which repeats the top margin at every chunk boundary; ours puts the sides on the chunks and the ends on the column that holds them.

**The rule that decides restyle from redraw** is that indent and spacing become pixel spans when the text is built, so changing either, or changing the text size while either is set, owes a redraw (`ParagraphShape.needsRedrawFor`, mutation-tested). **The exemption in it is the point:** with both at zero a text-size drag restyles the views it already has rather than re-parsing the chapter per slider step, where tsundoku routes the same change through a full content reload of every loaded chapter.

**The three gesture settings turned up two defects the plan did not predict.** Auto-scroll was dead in the shared host's WebView mode too, not only the native one, because the only start and stop calls lived in the legacy screen's composable. **The injected scroller was running at about a tenth of the speed asked for, in visible jerks, and had been since it was written**: the vendored CSS set `scroll-behavior: smooth`, so each of the sixty `scrollBy` calls a second started an animation the next one interrupted. It scrolls at a rate now rather than a fixed step per frame, which had also run at double speed on a 120Hz display, and the unit is a CSS pixel, so the native viewport multiplies by display density (measured at 2.0x: native 362 px/s against WebView 366, intended 360). **Turning text selection on had left the chrome unreachable**, because a selectable TextView gives its touches to the Editor and fires no click; taps are read from an `OnItemTouchListener` above the recycler's children now, with exactly one tap owner per mode.

### The seam's own rule, and step 7's walk

**The chrome naming the chapter above the one on screen was a rule written twice.** The native renderer puts the seam marker inside the chapter's own item view, so a reader stopped in a seam is in the chapter below it (`boundsOf` keeps the geometry to the text alone), while the WebView engine attributed the seam's pixels to the chapter above. Measured on the emulator: at a backward load `scrollY` was 8164 against an open chapter starting at 8188.05, a gap of exactly the `#rk-chapters` top padding. `rebuildBoundaries` records a `claimFrom` per chapter now, the top of the seam that introduces it, while progress still measures from the chapter's own start. Pinned by `aReaderInsideASeamIsInTheChapterBelowIt` and `aPrependAtTheTopDoesNotRenameTheChapterBeingRead`, both of which the reader audit moved into `TextViewportContractTest` so they run against both renderers. A first attempt patched the prepend compensation instead, which fixed one symptom and was reverted.

**Step 7c's walk found four defects rather than none, which is why the walk exists.** Bionic reading was a one-way toggle, since nothing un-applied the replaced text nodes; the emphasis is a class on the root now, with the stylesheet making the spans inert. The swipe threshold was a quarter of the viewport with no origin rule, where `core.js` and the native renderer both use 180 CSS px plus the start-on-the-far-half rule. A settings push landing while a document loads was dropped, and queues like a window verb now. And a pipeline setting changed after a seamless crossing reopened the chapter the session started on, because `load()` re-aims at `pendingChapterId`, which only an explicit open moves.

**"Remove extra spacing" did nothing in the native renderer**, which the WebView mode made visible by doing something: `collapseBlankLines` flattened every run of newlines to one, and a `<br>` run is exactly what the setting trims, so both states rendered identically. One break per run is taken now, and a surviving blank line no longer picks up paragraph spacing.

**Bionic reading had no in-reader control on the shared host** (owner, 2026-09-10, ruled in during the walk): `onClickBionic` was supplied only by the legacy screen, so the bar button was unreachable in both new modes. It is a `ReaderBionicReading` capability answered by the novel provider and null for manga, mirroring `ReaderAutoScroll`. That is a 7f-class find made before 7f: the takeover had dropped an affordance the replaced reader had.

## Decisions & tradeoffs

- **The host is one Activity and the engine sits above two providers.** The program's usual shape does not survive contact with a window-owning host, and pretending it does is how this surface would get mis-planned.
- **Not diverging the image viewers further is what keeps this from being Option A**, and it is the first thing to check when a step feels like it is fighting the design. The rule is stated as the enumerated mechanisms in content-layer.md's Reader row rather than as "byte-identical" or a marker count: the files already carried markers before the takeover, and both numbers this doc tried to freeze them at were wrong within a week.
- **`WebGpuConfig` reads the crop-borders setting of the mode its viewer is serving** (owner-ruled island). One viewer class serves both the paged and the long-strip modes, and it read the paged key in both, so the control acted on nothing in long strip. Recorded here because it is one of the enumerated divergences and a sync will meet it.
- **`WebGpuViewer.getPage` drops a cached page wrapper whose `ReaderPage` was replaced** (owner-ruled island, 2026-09-18, from the reload fixes after the 0.4.0 range audit). The viewer caches wrappers by chapter id and page index, and a chapter loaded again keeps both while its pages are new objects, so reloading the open chapter kept drawing the old pages, and a reload from the source turned the ones not yet decoded into errors, since their cached images had just been deleted. The wrapper is now current only while it holds the very page asked for (`isCachedPageCurrent` in `WebGpuPageCache.kt`, pinned by `WebGpuPageCacheTest`); a stale one is removed and cleaned up as an eviction is. `setChapters`' chapter-jump island asks the same rule before keeping the page on screen, so a reload lands on its requested page. Every lookup from a `ReaderPage` goes through `getPage`, the neighbour walks included, so the same check closes upstream's own case of a chapter released and opened again while the viewer still held its pages. The alternative, rebuilding the viewer on every reload from the host, was declined: it touched all three viewers and would repeat the host's reading-mode setup on each reload.
- **Position is typed, not an `Int`, with one named exception.** Sharing an integer page count between a paged medium and a continuous one is the derive-or-do-not-own defect in its most tempting form: the storage would be shared and the meaning forked. The exception is `ReaderChapter.requestedPage`, and it is an exception precisely because honouring the rule there would mean editing the three image viewers, which costs more than it buys.
- **The tsundoku port is the two viewers' portable layers, not their reader.** Their `NovelViewer` calls twenty-one distinct members of their forked `ReaderActivity`, and their host carries roughly fifty `is NovelViewer || is NovelWebViewViewer` type checks, so taking either viewer whole would mean taking their fork of the file we are least willing to fork. That type-check sprawl is also the concrete cost of the design Shape C was chosen over, and it is worth reading before anyone proposes instanceof branching in the host again. The renderer, the WebView stylers and the shared pipeline underneath are domain-free and are the parts worth having.
- **Their engine, not ours and not a new one.** Keeping the LNReader-derived reader was rejected because its engine is a vendored file nobody can update, which is the reason the migration exists. A from-scratch renderer was rejected because it has no upstream to sync from, and text rendering is where the already-solved hard problems are. This is the same maintainability-via-upstream argument that justifies the Mihon base.
- **The seam falls below their viewers, not through them**, and that is what keeps this a port rather than a bolt-on. The measurement decided it: their forward-only chapter queue appears 115 times across the two viewers and not once in any layer beneath them. Below that line the code is domain-free and tested; above it, it is welded to a data model where novels are manga rows.
- **Their reader is a source, not a base.** Tsundoku has no novel tables at all, so everything of theirs that touches persistence encodes novels-as-manga, which Reikai rejected at the data layer. That is not a flaw in their app, it is a different decision, and it is the reason the port stops where it does.
- **The WebView mode is a capability slot, not a hedge.** A text layout cannot render a source's own CSS faithfully, which is a named mechanism, so the mode exists for the sources that need it rather than as insurance against the native renderer failing.
- **We keep our TTS transport even though we are taking their engine.** The two halves were measured separately, and each side won one.
- **Accepted sync cost.** This is the highest-churn surface the program has taken over, on the least diverged files, and the amendment says so rather than borrowing the library's sunk-tax argument. Every future upstream reader fix becomes a manifest-guarded triage on the orchestration we own, while the viewers and the engine floor keep porting mechanically, which is the boundary that keeps the cost bounded.
- **The in-reader sheet collapse waits for the cutover and for the extras** (step 11) rather than leading, because folding novels into the host changes what the two sheets are. The app-level settings screen did not wait, because it was orthogonal, and it shipped as step 1.
- **The settings inventory is part of the takeover, not a follow-up.** A takeover is judged by what the replaced code did, and on this surface a large part of what it did was implement settings inside a vendored JS file. Leaving that to a separate roadmap item would have shipped a reader that silently lost font choice and themes, which is the exact failure the behaviour-inventory bar exists to catch.
- **The mark-read threshold is the incumbent 97%**, not tsundoku's 95 or 99. Changing a threshold nobody complained about would be a behaviour change smuggled in under a migration. It became the default of a novel setting at step 11 rather than a fixed number, which is the slider this bullet once declined; the number a user never touches is still 97.
- **The latest chapter switch wins in both readers, by two mechanisms rather than one kernel** (owner, 2026-09-15, reader audit finding C13). Manga's switches run as overlapping background jobs, so `ChapterSwitches` decides which may land: a page switch that anything newer overtook is dropped, and a pick, a step or the first open always lands. Novels already serialize switches under the `lane` lock and drop an open that a later one overtook (`NovelReaderViewModel.load`). The shared part would be a one-line comparison over inputs of different shapes, so the rule is recorded here instead of forced into a kernel; a change to either half re-reads the other. The bookmark and link stay on the active chapter: with the race closed it matches the chapter on screen except for the moment a switch takes.
- **Read aloud can mark and step by sentence, off by default** (owner, 2026-09-17). Highlight sentence, shown under Highlight paragraph, speaks each sentence as its own utterance and marks it as the engine starts it (`onStart` on per-piece utterance ids), rather than trusting `onRangeStart`, which many third-party engines never call. The paragraph stays the default, since some engines pause between utterances. The controller asks the engine for the paragraph's pieces (`NovelTtsEngine.pieces`), so it knows the sentences it steps between: Next and Previous move one sentence, crossing into the neighbouring paragraph's first or last one at either end, and Play after a pause carries on from the paused sentence. Positions, the notification seek and relocation stay paragraph-based. A sentence is located in each renderer by its non-space characters, the rule both already find paragraphs by (`ChunkParagraph.chunkRange` natively, `part` in `reader.js`). The whole paragraph is not marked before the first sentence starts, or it flashes.
- **The in-reader sheet has a Read aloud tab for novels** (owner, 2026-09-17), reversing step 11's line that the sheet holds only what is adjusted while looking at the page, under which step 8 had moved every read-aloud row to the settings screen. It carries the engine and voice-language pickers too, as the deleted reader's TTS tab and LNReader's do, although a new engine stops playback. The live engine and voice lookup is one `rememberTtsOptions` both screens call.
- **The novel sheet is grouped under headings and reaches the settings a viewport is built with** (owner, 2026-09-17). Reading holds this series, Text, Paragraphs and Margins; Appearance holds Theme, Page and Chapter text; Controls holds Scrolling, Gestures and Navigation; Read aloud holds Voice, Playback and Highlight. Rendering mode, long-press text selection and the two WebView font settings were kept off the sheet because nothing rebuilt a novel viewport mid-session. They are on it now, and a change to any of them recreates the Activity around the live session (`NovelReaderProvider.viewportRebuilds`), which lands where the reader is, as a rotation does. The rendering mode also joined the chapter loader's pipeline snapshot, since it decides the render target the cached chapter was built for.
- **The next chapter joins the window at a percent the reader sets** (owner, 2026-09-17, overruling the 2026-09-08 "fixed constant" line, which matched no code: the window added the next chapter as soon as it was fetched, on open). `readerAutoLoadNextAt`, 95 by default, gates only the append, through `NovelWindowReach.joinable`: the fetch still starts on open, since the next-chapter button and read aloud reaching a chapter's end both wait on it, so the setting saves no data. A chapter that fits on one screen never reports past 0 and lets the reach join at once, and a chapter already published stays whatever the percent, because taking it back out from below the reader would move the page. Novel-only: manga's image viewers trigger their next chapter a fixed five pages from the end, inside the viewer classes this surface does not diverge further, and the seamless window is itself a novel-only capability.
- **The gear is an ordered button and the progress navigator can be hidden** (owner, 2026-09-17, reversing "gear fixed last"). `ReaderBottomButton.Settings` is declared last, so a stored order that predates it draws it last as before, and `ordered` always keeps it, so no selection can lose the way into the sheet; the editor lists it to drag with its checkbox disabled. Hiding the navigator is `ReaderNavigatorShape.None`, which each provider answers from its own switch (`ReaderPreferences.showNavigator`, `NovelPreferences.readerShowNavigator`, a second boolean beside the novel rail switch rather than a migrated three-state key). With it, neither rail nor slider is drawn and the action row draws the chapter buttons at its two ends, swapped for a right-to-left reader as the horizontal navigator swaps them. Both types, since nothing about the content separates them.
- **Both readers can reload the open chapter** (owner, 2026-09-17), as tsundoku's reader menu can: **Reload chapter** and **Reload chapter from source**, through `ReaderProvider.reloadChapter`. A novel reuses the in-place reload a chapter-text setting already runs, dropping the chapter from the session's text cache, and from the source it skips the downloaded copy once (`NovelChapterTextLoader.load`). A manga chapter is reloaded by resetting its state and page loader and re-sending the viewer chapters at the page on screen; from the source it skips the download and drops the chapter's cached page list and images first, which tsundoku does not, so its "from source" can serve the cached copy again. Neither rewrites a downloaded copy.
- **A rebuilt renderer lands on the line the reader had at the top** (owner batch, 2026-09-17). The percent a rotation used to restore moved the top line by a paragraph once the width changed, and within a chapter's last screen every position read 100. Both renderers now report the top line as the count of characters before it in the chapter, spaces, pictures and ruby readings left out (`shownCharCount`, and `topLine` in `reader.js`, which count alike so a rendering-mode switch lands close too), the model keeps the latest (`reportTopLine`), and `landingOf` hands it over as `LoadedChapter.topLine`; native lands it as `Landing.Text`, the page before its ready report. The line is the one crossing the screen's top edge, measured at the edge itself rather than below the cutout inset: a rebuilt page gets the inset after it lands, and measured below it each portrait rotation moved the reader a line on. Rotating also exposed a race that lost any landing, percent included: a previous chapter joining above a chapter the list had not laid out yet took the screen, so `join` now starts the list on the chapter still headed for its landing. Pinned by `aChapterRebuiltAtALineLandsThatLineAtTheTop` and `aChapterRebuiltAtALineStillReportsThatLineOnceTheInsetArrives`, both seen red; walked on the emulator over six rotations in each mode with no drift.
- **Native corrections run before the frame is drawn** (owner, 2026-09-17). Every hold of the line at the top in the native renderer goes through `postCorrection`: a seam or failure view turning up above the text (`keepReaderStill`), a restyle (`holdingReader`) and images landing. Posted, it ran after the frame, and that frame drew the reader moved, 1440 px when the chapter above joined and 9735 px on a line-height change in the emulator tests, for as long as the frame took to draw (about half a second on the emulator after a rotation). It now runs from a pre-draw listener: `ViewRootImpl.performTraversals` lays out, then dispatches pre-draw, then draws, and `RecyclerView.scrollBy` moves and fills rows synchronously, so the frame already shows the corrected position. This is the order the WebView page already had, since its `place` corrects from a resize observer. A detached list keeps the post, having nothing to draw. The posted landings and follows (the open, a redraw's rebuild, read-aloud following its text) are deliberately left: they move the reader somewhere new rather than holding a line already on screen. Pinned by `noFrameShowsTheReaderMovedWhenTheSeamAboveTheTextTurnsUp` and `noFrameShowsTheReaderMovedWhenTheLineHeightChanges`, which record the line on every drawn frame and failed 3 of 3 before.
- **A rebuilt renderer lands before its first frame, and a line does not wait for pictures** (owner, 2026-09-17). After a rotation the native renderer drew the chapter from its start for a frame (the landed line 27330 px down in the test) before landing, because `join` posted the landing. It now lands through `beforeNextDraw`, the hook `postCorrection` uses. A line landing (`Landing.Text`) also waited for the chapter's pictures like a share, leaving the chapter start on screen for as long as they took; a line needs no final height, and once landed a picture arriving above it is growth the correction holds, so it lands at once. `reader.js` made the same wait for a line and held its ready report with it, and now lands a line at once too. A share still waits for its pictures in both renderers, since it is a fraction of the final height, so opening a chapter at a saved percent still shows the chapter start while they load; hiding the text until it lands would be the fix, and is not ruled. The posted re-lands after pictures arrive, after a redraw and the read-aloud retry are left: they move a view already showing the start, or jump on purpose. Pinned by `noFrameShowsAChapterRebuiltAtALineBeforeItLands`, `noFrameShowsAChapterRebuiltAtALineWhileAPictureAboveItLoads` and the contract case `aChapterRebuiltAtALineLandsBeforeAPictureAboveItArrives`, each seen red first.
