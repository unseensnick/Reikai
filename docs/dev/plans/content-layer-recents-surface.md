# Content-layer recents surface (history, updates and the combined feed)

> **Standing rules for every session working this surface.** The program rules bind here and are not restated: they live in [.claude/rules/content-layer.md](../../../.claude/rules/content-layer.md), which loads every session (write-once, typed capability slots over nullable soup, derive-or-do-not-own, the pin-once ladder, decline expiry). For the program context read [content-layer-architecture.md](content-layer-architecture.md) and [unified-content-ui.md](unified-content-ui.md) first. Two more bind on this surface specifically. **All-first**, taken from the library ([library-all-chip.md](library-all-chip.md)): All is the real feed and the Manga and Novels chips are predicates over it, so anything describing the list or a row in it is one value, and only what describes a content type stays per type. And **every keyed structure keys on `EntryId`**, never a raw `Long`: the two id spaces overlap, so a `Long`-keyed map over a mixed feed cross-wires silently instead of crashing. The surfaces being replaced broke the first rule at the state level (two search queries, two loading flags, two dialog channels and two selections on one screen) and paid for it with a clear-all that prompts twice, a novel feed that gates the manga chip's spinner, and a long-press range that selects rows the user never swept.

## Goal

One Reikai-owned recent-activity surface serving manga and light novels across four modes: a combined title-centric feed, a combined grouped digest, Updates, and History. A Settings preference collapses the separate Updates and History tabs into one Recents tab holding those modes; with the preference off (the default), today's two tabs remain, rendering the same engine at two of its modes.

This is the fifth surface of the content-layer program ([content-layer-architecture.md](content-layer-architecture.md)) and its third orchestration takeover, after the library and the migrate flow. It supersedes the forward half of [unified-updates.md](unified-updates.md), which stays the record of the shipped unified Updates tab.

## Why

Two forces meet on these two screens, and doing either alone reworks the other.

**The behaviour is still forked.** History and Updates were built when the program's seam was drawn at the UI leaf, so they share row composables over per-type feeds and nothing below. The measured shape of the fork: the novel Updates feed filters nothing in SQL where the manga feed filters four axes; both feeds resolve category membership per row in Kotlin behind a screen-lifetime cache, on both types; the "started" predicate disagrees with its SQL twin in the negated branch; and list-level state (search, loading, dialogs, selection, clear-all) is stored twice, once per content type, on a single screen. These are duplicate-implementation defects, the failure mode the program's 2026-08-06 ruling exists to remove.

**The combined feed reshapes the same screens.** `unseensnick/Reikai#57` asks for a title-centric feed merging recently read, recently updated and newly added, opening the most relevant chapter, with Updates and History preserved. The owner's answer is a preference that collapses the two tabs into one Recents tab with modes inside, so nobody loses one-tap access. Building the behaviour seam first and the tab shape second would rework both, so they are one piece of work.

**The sync cost is the lowest of any surface taken over so far.** Twelve months of upstream history on both screens: History took five commits, every one cross-cutting mechanical work (the datetime conversion, the ViewModel migration, a compose-workaround removal, the immutable-collections drop, preference properties) and none behaviour-specific; Updates took thirteen, of which three are behaviour (`mihonapp/mihon#3589`, the earlier filters commit, and a selection-signature cleanup) and the rest mechanical or revert churn.

## Approach

### The amendment: scope and hard lines

This is the fourth orchestration amendment to the program, recorded alongside the others in [content-layer-architecture.md](content-layer-architecture.md). It supersedes the history-and-updates part of the 2026-08-06 "redo at the behaviour seam" ruling by naming what the redo actually is.

- **The takeover owns pure UI plus orchestration**: the two Reikai shells (`ReikaiUpdatesScreen`, `ReikaiHistoryScreen`) and their row builders, the two Reikai novel models (`NovelUpdatesViewModel`, `NovelHistoryViewModel`, which dissolve into providers), the bodies of the two Voyager tabs, and the category-filter control (`ReikaiUpdatesCategoryFilter`). The shared row leaves (`EntryUpdatesRow`, `EntryHistoryRow`) survive into the new engine rather than being replaced.
- **It also reaches, more lightly, into shell and plumbing files** the bullet above does not cover, and they are named so the scope is not a surprise mid-build: `ReikaiSourcePreferences` and `CategoryIdPreferences` (the retiring keys), `PreferenceRestorer` (their skips), `UpdatesFilterDialog` and `UpdatesSettingsViewModel` (the filter sheet), `historyView.sq` (a Reikai-only category predicate, since History carries a selection of its own), `CategoryFilterRow` (the shared picker's confirm, which also serves the library), `UiPreferences` and the Appearance settings screen (the new tab preference), and `HomeScreen` plus `MainActivity` (the tab set and the launcher shortcuts).
- **The engine floor stays Mihon and synced**, never reimplemented: `GetUpdates`, `UpdatesRepository` and its impl, `GetHistory`, `RemoveHistory`, `GetNextChapters`, `SetReadStatus`, `DownloadManager`, and every interactor below them. The novel sinks (`SetNovelReadStatus`, `GetNextNovelChapter`, the novel repositories, `NovelDownloadCache`) stay likewise. A step that starts reimplementing what `setReadStatus` does has gone too far.
- **`UpdatesViewModel` and `HistoryViewModel` stay live** as the manga providers, minimally patched, and are not manifested. That is this amendment's ruling, not an inherited default: the library takeover's equivalent clause is recorded as unresolved in [content-layer.md](../../../.claude/rules/content-layer.md), and the churn measured above gives no case for absorbing these two.
- **Replaced Mihon files are deleted and manifested in the same commit**, which the `pre-commit` hook enforces. See "What can and cannot be deleted", which also covers the two existing manifest rows this takeover invalidates.
- **The reader-adjacent verbs are out of scope.** Resume opens a chapter through the existing readers; nothing about reader behaviour changes here.

### All-first, taken from the library

The library's ruling ([library-all-chip.md](library-all-chip.md)) is adopted whole. That doc states it as "the chip becomes a predicate, not a branch", which is about the assembly algorithm; the mechanism underneath is worth stating separately, because it is what `LibraryEngine` actually does and it is easy to reimplement wrongly.

- **Every provider's flow always runs and is always collected. The chip then selects which providers' rows enter the assembly, and the assembly algorithm itself is chip-blind.** Nothing is gated at collection time, which is the property that matters.
- **This is about content types, not lanes.** Which lanes an engine collects is decided by the modes it renders: an engine rendering only Updates collects only the updated lane. That is not a chip branch and does not weaken the rule above, and it is what stops the two-tab shape running every query twice, since with the preference off each tab holds its own engine instance.
- **Anything describing the list is one value.** One search query, one loading flag, one dialog channel, one selection, one clear-all confirmation. Anything describing a content type stays per type: the feed queries, the download-state source, the scanlator predicate.
- **The assembly output is chip-tagged**, because the flow lags a chip flip by one emission and the screen must render an assembly only when the tag matches.
- **Counts follow the active chip**, computed over the filtered rows. **An empty section is always hidden**, no bare header.
- **Providers keep their own filtering.** They read different tables through different repositories, so filtering at the provider is honest; only assembly is shared.
- **Two deliberate departures from the template**, both because this surface has state the library does not. The library's engine forwards a search query to the providers and then reads it back out of provider state; here the engine **owns** the one query string and fans it out, because All-first says a value describing the list is one value and reading it back from two provider states is the shape that produced the two-search-fields defect. And the library derives only `searchActive` and the counts over the active providers, because it has nothing else list-shaped; here loading, emptiness and the last-updated line are derived the same way, which is what stops the novel feed gating the manga chip's spinner.

### Ruled design picks (owner, 2026-08-08)

1. **Four modes**, matching Yokai's set: a grouped digest, a flat combined feed, Updates, and History. Cut to three later if the digest does not earn its place; because modes are render policies over one item stream, that is deleting a policy rather than unpicking a design.
2. **The flat combined feed is what the request asked for.** `unseensnick/Reikai#57` describes one main entry per title, deduplicated by title, sorted by latest activity, opening the most relevant chapter, which is Yokai's `UngroupedAll` (whose own label is "All"). The digest is Yokai's `GroupedAll`, a different thing: at most four new-chapter rows, then continue-reading rows up to a combined nine across those two sections, then at most four newly-added, each under its own header.
3. **Mode names are placeholders** until they can be seen in the app. Nothing downstream depends on the strings.
4. **The combined modes always collapse merged series.** The existing `updates_group_by_series` preference survives, but only as the Updates mode's toggle; it does not gate the combined modes, which have no correct ungrouped reading.
5. **Newly-added respects the category filter but not the chapter-state filters.** A newly-added row has no chapter, so unread, started and bookmarked have nothing to test.
6. **No paging.** Each lane keeps its own bound (see below). Yokai pages every mode except the digest at fifty rows with a recursive top-up loop, and that is what forces cross-page deduplication bookkeeping.
7. **The whole Yokai affordance set is in scope** except paging: swipe actions, a per-row download button with its visibility preference, read-progress display, History's time grouping, and search within the surface.
8. **The category filter is one selection per rendered surface, covering both content types** (revised 2026-08-08, the same revision ruling 10 made to the chip and for the same reason; the first cut said one selection for the whole surface). Each rendered surface gets a master toggle, an include set and an exclude set: Updates, History, and the combined Recents tab, nine keys. The four per-type category keys and the old `updates_filter_categories` are dropped with a restore skip and no value carry-over, matching how the library retired its per-type filter keys. That retirement is why the live master switches carry an `_enabled` suffix. All six id sets register in `CategoryIdPreferences`' `sharedSets` bucket, so every cleanup path picks them up.
   - **What was right in the first cut, and survives:** with the combined tab on there is one selection over one list, which is what makes the combined modes coherent, since a feed that filters only one of its three lanes by category is not filtered by category. What was wrong is what happens with the preference off, where Updates and History are two tabs that look and behave independently and one selection would make each silently move the other.
   - **The alternative is worse than it looks.** One shared selection means History inherits whatever was set on Updates, and History has no control of its own until step 8, so a user with the preference off gets a silently shortened History and nowhere to see or change it. Per surface, History is simply unfiltered until it has a control.
   - **The shared picker's confirm becomes a delta write**, fixed once in `CategoryFilterRow` rather than per caller (step 1a). Today it replaces the whole stored set with the tri-state map it was handed, and that map only covers the categories that section displayed, so any surface showing a subset silently drops the ids it did not show. A delta write, `(stored minus the shown ids) plus the confirmed ids`, makes that impossible for every caller.
   - **This fixes a live bug in the library**, which already has one shared pair and a chip-scoped list: select categories under All, switch to the Manga chip, press OK in the picker without changing anything, and every novel-only category id is dropped from the filter. It fires in either direction and is silent. The library is a different surface, but this is the component this step is already changing, so the fix lands with it rather than being flagged and left.
   - **Each picker shows the union of all categories** regardless of the chip. With the delta write in place that is a display choice rather than a correctness requirement, and it is the right one here: a surface's selection spans both libraries, so hiding half of it per chip would misrepresent what is stored. The library keeps its per-chip list, which suits a surface whose chip scopes what you are looking at.
9. **The master toggle survives.** Its job is to park a selection without applying it, and the library carries the identical switch (`pref_filter_library_categories`). Retiring it here would make the same control behave differently on two surfaces and would force a no-master mode into the shared `CategoryFilterRow`, to buy alignment with an upstream preference this plan does not adopt anyway.
10. **The chip is per rendered surface, so there are three keys, not one** (revised 2026-08-08 after the first ruling proved wrong in the two-tab shape). With the preference on there is one Recents tab, one chip strip and one new key. With it off there are two tabs that look and behave independently, and linking their chips would move something for people who never opted in, against the whole point of defaulting the preference off. So `updates_content_type` and `history_content_type` stay, and the Recents tab gets its own third key. Flipping the preference back and forth then restores what each side had. `stats_content_type` is a different surface and is untouched.
11. **Tracker push stays on the details screens.** Marking read here will not push progress, matching both what these screens do today and what upstream's Updates tab does.

### Feed bounds

Each lane keeps its own bound and the combined modes are the deduplicated union of whatever the active lanes return. "Bounded" below means bounded in the query; the read lane is bounded by its own shape instead.

- **Updated lane**: three months and five hundred rows, as today.
- **Read lane**: no `LIMIT`, because the SQL already reduces it to one row per entry through the `max_last_read` subquery, so its size is bounded by how many entries have ever been read.
- **Added lane**: needs an explicit limit, since nothing bounds it naturally. Match the updated lane's five hundred unless measurement says otherwise.

### Deduplication, stated once

There is one rule: **one row per merge group, falling back to one row per entry for an entry that is in no group.** The survivor is the most recent, which the kernel gets by ordering before it collapses; the lanes each arrive newest-first but their union does not, so nothing falls out for free. The kernel implements the per-entry half; step 6 upgrades it to per-group by resolving series keys, changing the kernel's output rather than sitting beside it.

**The scope of the collapse belongs to the render policy, not the kernel** (ruled 2026-08-08 while building step 3). The flat modes collapse across every lane, so one title is one row. The digest collapses within each lane, so a series read today and updated today appears under both continue-reading and new-chapters. Updates does not collapse at all, since its grouping is a display toggle. Yokai collapses globally before it sections, and pays for it: the survivor is whichever lane's timestamp is larger, the section that lost the row is short against its cap, and which section loses is decided by a comparison the user cannot see. Keeping the scope out of the kernel is what avoids porting that.

### The target chapter is decided per lane

The row a user sees and the chapter a tap opens are different things, and the rule is the lane's, not one global winner. This is why the item model separates them.

- **Read lane**: resume where you were, which is Mihon's existing history semantics (reopen the recorded chapter if it is unfinished, otherwise the next one). Do not "upgrade" this to the library's next-unread rule; they encode different intents and both are correct where they are.
- **Updated lane**: the first unread chapter of the update burst, Yokai's rule (the first unread chapter fetched within twelve hours of the row's own chapter), falling back to the row's own chapter.
- **Added lane**: the first unread chapter.

Per content type the mechanics stay behind the provider: manga resolves through `GetNextChapters` with the scanlator filter, novels through `GetNextNovelChapter` in source order. Both are merge-unaware today; step 6 closes that.

**Resolution is lazy, per rendered row.** Resolving eagerly at assembly means one chapter query per row per emission, which on a five-hundred-row feed is the cost Yokai paid for the same feature. Resolve when a row is rendered, cache by `EntryId` for the assembly's life, and invalidate with the assembly. If a row's subtitle needs the resolved chapter's name rather than the recorded one, that is the case to measure before committing to lazy.

### What can and cannot be deleted

Checked against [off-path-manifest.md](../off-path-manifest.md), whose `pre-commit` hook requires that no manifested path exists in the working tree **and that every `Replacement` exists**.

- **Two live rows name files this takeover deletes, and must be repointed in the same commit**: `UpdatesScreen.kt` currently points at `ReikaiUpdatesScreen.kt`, and `HistoryviewModelStateProvider.kt` points at `ReikaiHistoryScreen.kt`. Both replacements go away at the cutover, so both rows move to the new engine's file. Miss this and every subsequent commit fails the hook.
- **Two rows are unaffected**: `HistoryItem.kt` and `HistoryWithRelationsProvider.kt` point at `EntryHistoryRow`, which survives.
- **Explicitly ruled live and unlisted, do not delete**: `eu/kanade/presentation/history/HistoryScreen.kt` still holds `HistoryUiModel`, which Mihon's own model emits and the shared screen consumes, and `eu/kanade/presentation/updates/UpdatesUiItem.kt` still holds the type-neutral last-updated line. The manifest records both as partially collapsed. If the takeover removes the last live symbol from either, that is when its row gets added.
- **Genuine candidates**: `UpdatesDeleteConfirmationDialog.kt` and `eu/kanade/presentation/history/components/HistoryDialogs.kt` at the cutover; `UpdatesFilterDialog.kt` only once the mode-scoped sheet actually replaces it, which is the affordance step, so it is deleted and manifested there rather than at the cutover.
- **Note that `EntryUpdatesRow` has no manifest row**, unlike its history twin. Nothing is wrong with that (its Mihon original is still partially live), but do not assume symmetry.

### What the requester already has

Two ingredients are shipped and must not be rebuilt: per-title collapsing (the Updates "Group by series" toggle, merge-aware) and manga-plus-novel interleaving on both screens. History additionally deduplicates to one row per entry in SQL already, through the `max_last_read` subquery in `historyView` and its novel twin. What is genuinely new is combining the three activity lanes, a newly-added query per content type, search on the Updates lane (which has none today), and the conditional tab set.

### Known defects in the surfaces being replaced

Recorded so the takeover fixes them deliberately rather than carrying them across. Verified in current code; each names its owning step, or says it is deferred or ruled.

- Clear-all under the All chip sets the delete-all dialog on both models, so the user confirms twice and gets two snackbars. **Step 7.**
- The novel feed's loading flag is unconditional in the shared loading expression, so the novel first emission gates the Manga chip's spinner. **Step 4**, by deriving loading over the active providers.
- Long-press range selection exists only for manga and fills its range using indices into the manga model's own list, which is not the rendered order once the All chip interleaves or grouping collapses. Novels have no range selection at all. **Step 7**, which is sequenced after the render policies precisely because "the rendered order" does not exist until then.
- The "started" filter's negated branch disagreed between SQL and the novel Kotlin path: SQL requires unstarted and unread, the Kotlin negation let read chapters through. **Closed in step 1**, by writing the novel SQL twin to the manga branch's shape (1b) and deleting the Kotlin branch (1c).
- The excluded-scanlators switch renders unconditionally and is a silent no-op on the Novels chip, which the capability-slot rule forbids. **Step 8**, as a typed slot hidden for novels.
- History resume is merge-unaware on both content types. **Step 6**, which changes each provider's resume alongside the collapse it enables.
- Tab-reselect resume on History compares manga-latest against novel-latest regardless of the active chip, so it can open a manga from the Novels chip. **Step 10**, with the rest of the reselect decision. Step 8 preserves today's reselect behaviour unchanged so the cutover moves one thing at a time.
- History search runs on the raw SQL title while the row displays a custom override, so a renamed entry cannot be found by its displayed name. **Closed in step 7**, by matching the row's own title in the engine instead of in SQL; the row already carries the override, so no overlay holder is needed here. This was History only: the Updates surface had no search at all.
- Novel update rows carry no live download progress; the provider hardcodes zero where manga rows merge the download manager's status and progress flows. **Deferred to Road B**, which owns the download-state reconciliation. The row keeps the honest zero rather than faking progress.
- Marking a chapter read from either feed pushes nothing to trackers, and **that is kept** (ruled above). Listed so a future session does not "fix" it into a divergence from upstream.
- The two engines' group chapter lists are ordered opposite ways and the shared target rules assumed one of them, so a merged manga resumed the chapter before the one just finished. Found by the 2026-08-09 write-once audit, **closed in step 8a**; it never reached a user, since nothing renders the engine yet.
- Dead code to remove with the surfaces: `NovelHistoryViewModel` injects a merge manager it never references, `UpdatesViewModel.showConfirmDeleteChapters` has no caller (making its dialog branch unreachable), and its `InternalError` event is never sent though the tab still consumes it. **Step 8.**
- Deliberate and kept: the manga feed bounds on chapter upload date while the novel feed bounds on fetch date, because many light-novel sources leave upload date at zero. The reason is recorded in the novel view's own header comment and stays a typed divergence.

## Sequenced steps

Step 1 lands in the live screens and its fixes are visible immediately: filtering moves into SQL, the started negation closes, and the stale membership cache goes. Steps 2 through 7 are then compile-gated and render nothing, because the existing shells stay on screen until step 8 swaps them. That is deliberate (it is how the details and migrate surfaces were cut over), and it means every defect owned by a step after 1 stays live for the user until the cutover.

1. **The data floor**, in three ordered parts.
   - **1a, the preferences.** Introduce a master toggle plus an include and an exclude set per rendered surface, the sets typed `Preference<Set<String>>` and all six registered in the `sharedSets` bucket of `CategoryIdPreferences` (see the decision below on why not upstream's `List<Long>` shape), and the Recents tab's own chip key. Retire five keys with dead-key constants and a `PreferenceRestorer` skip, and no value carry-over: the four per-type category sets and `updates_filter_categories`. The two existing chip keys stay live, per ruling 10. Update `CategoryIdPreferences`' KDoc, which enumerates the buckets. **No cleanup migration and no `versionCode` bump**: a retired key's value lingers unread in the preferences XML, which is exactly how the library's sort, filter and group-by retirements left theirs. **Also in this part, convert `CategoryFilterRow`'s confirm from a replace to a delta write** (see ruling 8), which is a prerequisite for a shared pair behind a subset picker and simultaneously fixes the library's silent drop. Pin it with a test where the stored set holds an id the section never displays, and verify by mutation: restore the replace, watch the test go red.
   - **1b, the queries.** Port `mihonapp/mihon#3589` into `updatesView.sq` (the include and exclude predicates with their two empty-set booleans, uncategorized as sentinel id zero via `NOT EXISTS`). The `IN :collection` idiom is already in the tree, so it needs no proving. Add a **new** filtered novel query beside the existing `getRecentNovelUpdates` rather than changing it, because the unfiltered one has two other callers in the home-screen widget; select the same columns from the same view so the existing mapper and `NovelUpdateWithRelations` are unchanged. The new query carries the category predicate against `novels_categories` plus unread, started and bookmarked, with started written to the manga branch's shape so the negation divergence closes. Downloaded stays in Kotlin on both, because download state is not in the database. Add the same category predicate to both history queries, since History has a selection of its own. That half is a Reikai divergence with no upstream twin, and it does not stop at the `.sq`: parameters added to `history:` change `HistoryRepository` and `GetHistory` too, so the amendment's "engine floor stays Mihon" means those files keep their behaviour and their sync relationship while gaining a Reikai-only parameter, exactly as `GetUpdates` gained upstream's. Affordable because `historyView.sq` has taken no upstream behaviour change since it moved modules in 2023. Leave `getLatestHistory` and `getLatestNovelHistory` unfiltered: they back tab-reselect, and a filtered resume would jump to a different entry than the one the feed shows. Add a recently-added query per content type against the base tables rather than the library views, whose per-entry chapter-count aggregation a newly-added row has no use for, coalescing `date_added` on the novel side and carrying the category predicate too. Six queries in total gain it: two updates, two history, two recently-added. Index `novels_categories` on both columns: the `CREATE INDEX` statements go in **both** the `.sq` file (for fresh installs, which never run migrations) and a new `.sqm` (for existing ones), per the rule that index changes are not bundled. A SQLDelight migration needs no `versionCode` bump.
   - **1c, the consumption.** Point **all four** live models at the new queries, the two updates ones and the two history ones, since 1b gives every feed the predicate. Delete `applyReikaiCategoryFilter`, both screen-lifetime membership caches, and the novel Kotlin chapter-state predicates whose SQL twins now exist (the started negation closes by deleting the Kotlin branch, not merely by writing the SQL). The two chip keys are not touched here at all, since ruling 10 keeps them for the two-tab shape; only the Recents tab's own key is new, and nothing reads it until step 10. **This wiring is not throwaway**: the manga models stay live as the providers and the novel models reshape into providers, so the call sites move at most one seam. It also fixes a real wart, since re-categorizing a series while the screen is open currently does not reflect until reopen, on both content types. Two things to expect: `UpdatesViewModel`'s combine **arity changes**, because its category filter shares the fifth slot with the custom-info overlay and only the filter half leaves, so that island is rebuilt around the overlay rather than deleted; and `matchesCategoryFilter` loses both its Updates callers, surviving on its library one.
2. **The provider seam and both adapters.** A `RecentsProvider` extending a `RecentsBehavior`, modelled on `LibraryProvider` over `LibraryBehavior`, plus the manga and novel adapters that implement it over the live models. This step ends with two working providers emitting lane flows, not just an interface. The neutral item carries entry identity as an `EntryId`, a timestamp, and a lane; per-type payloads ride as opaque values the shared layer never inspects, the discipline the migrate flow's DTOs already use (plain `Any`, unwrapped only inside the owning adapter). Four things this description originally got wrong or left out, all settled while building it:
   - **The lane is a typed slot, not an enum beside a nullable chapter id.** Only two of the three lanes have a chapter, so `RecentsLane.Read` and `Updated` carry a `ChapterRef` and `Added` carries none. A nullable `Long` would have been the banned shape, making the absent case representable everywhere instead of only where it happens.
   - **Target-chapter resolution lives on the provider, not on the item.** Resolution is lazy per rendered row, and an item is built at assembly time, so a resolver stored per item would resolve eagerly or hold a closure over the provider. `RecentsProvider.targetChapter` is a suspend function taking the item.
   - **`RecentsBehavior` carries no state at all**, deliberately unlike `LibraryBehavior`, which exposes a state flow the library's engine reads a search query back out of. That is the shape this surface is being rebuilt to remove, so the seam gives a second copy nowhere to live.
   - **The newly-added lane has to be built end to end here**, since 1b wrote only its SQL: two row models, one Reikai-owned repository serving both content types, and DI. It is Reikai-owned rather than a patch on Mihon's manga repository because the lane has no upstream twin on either side.
3. **The assembly kernel, pinned by tests before it is wired.** Pure functions the way `assembleLibrary` is, with unit tests standing in for the models (which resolve Injekt at init and cannot run in a unit test). The kernel owns two algorithms, ordering and collapsing; the scope of the collapse, sectioning and caps belong to the render policies below. Nothing tags a lane: an item has carried its own since the adapters were written. Ordering is total, breaking a timestamp tie on the lane, then the content type, then the entry id, because a stable sort over a concatenation only preserves whichever order the lanes happened to arrive in and would let rows swap places between emissions. No inputs object: nothing about assembly is preference-driven. Verify each test by mutation: delete the clause it names, watch it go red, restore it.
4. **The engine flow.** Combine the providers' lane flows, apply the chip by selecting active providers, run the kernel, tag the output with the chip. Loading, emptiness and the last-updated line are derived here, over the active providers, and nowhere else. Every keyed structure keys on `EntryId`. Four things settled while building it:
   - **A lane flow carries its own loaded flag.** A lane mapped off a model's state emits the moment it is collected, with an empty list, because that state is seeded rather than opened by its first emission. So "has it emitted yet" reports loaded one tick after subscribe with nothing on screen, and `RecentsLaneRows` pairs the rows with the flag instead; a query-backed lane starts from a loading value.
   - **Whether a filter is narrowing the feed is the surface's question, not a provider's.** The chapter-state filters reach only the updated lane, so a provider-level flag would have History call itself filtered because of a filter set on Updates. The engine ORs the category half with the chapter-state half only where the updated lane renders, through a pure function so the rule is testable without a preference store.
   - **The engine owns the chip**, reading its own surface's key. The novel updates model's copy stays live until the cutover deletes it; both read one preference, so they cannot disagree.
   - **The engine orders and never collapses.** The collapse scope is a render policy's call (see Deduplication), so step 5 collapses at whatever scope its mode wants.
5. **The render policies**, the four modes over that one stream: flat (deduplicated, bounded), digest (lane-bucketed, capped, headed, with footers that jump to the single-lane modes), Updates, History. Each policy is a pure function pinned in the same test file as the kernel, and each chooses its own collapse scope (see Deduplication). Four things settled while building it:
   - **Yokai's caps are adopted whole**: four new-chapter rows, continue-reading up to nine minus that count (one budget across the two chapter sections, so a big burst cannot push reading off the screen), four newly-added. Its section ordering comes too: the three sections sort by whichever holds the newest row, rather than sitting in a fixed order.
   - **Its twelve-hour upload tiebreak does not.** Inside new chapters, Yokai compares upload date for chapters fetched within twelve hours of each other and fetch date otherwise. `novelUpdatesView` selects no upload column at all, by design, because many light-novel sources leave it at zero, so porting the rule would order one content type's rows by a clock the other does not have, invisibly. The section keeps the kernel's order.
   - **A footer goes on the two chapter sections only.** It jumps to that lane's single-lane mode, and newly-added has none. Yokai does the same, for what is presumably the same reason.
   - **Updates grouping is per series and per day**, collapsing at two or more chapters, which is what the live screen already does; the flat feed carries no date headers, since a title-centric feed with day headers is History again.
6. **Merge collapse.** Series keys resolve once for whichever lanes are showing, upgrading the kernel's per-entry deduplication to per-group and giving History collapsing it has never had. The same step makes each provider's resume merge-aware, since a collapsed row must open the group's next chapter rather than one member's. Assembly never passes the chip down to the merge repository, which rejects the mixed content type on every method that takes one. It lands in two commits, collapse then resume, because the two fail differently: a wrong collapse shows the wrong number of rows, a wrong resume opens the wrong chapter. What the collapse half settled:
   - **Membership is a map from `EntryId` to group id**, carried on the assembly rather than read beside it, so a policy can never pair one emission's rows with another's groups. It is empty while series merging is off, gated at the provider because `membershipChanges()` is the raw repository flow and does not gate itself.

## Decisions & tradeoffs

- **All-first rather than a mode-scoped chip.** Chosen by the owner after the library proved it. The alternative, letting the chip decide what gets collected, is what produces the class of defect this surface already has: state that describes one list stored once per content type, with the two copies free to disagree.
- **Four modes, cut down later rather than added later.** The digest and the flat feed answer different questions and neither substitutes for the other, and the flat one is what was actually requested. Building both as render policies over one stream makes the reduction cheap and the addition-later expensive, so the risk is taken in the direction that is reversible.
- **The combined feed is bounded, not paged.** Yokai's endless paging is the source of its cross-page deduplication bookkeeping. A bounded feed keeps the deduplication a single pass over a known set.
- **One shared category-filter set rather than two per-type sets.** Category ids are one space: the schema unification shifted every novel category id by a fixed offset precisely so it cannot collide with a manga id, so per-type sets encode a distinction the data does not have. This follows the library's identical ruling.
- **The shared pair keeps Reikai's `Set<String>` shape rather than adopting upstream's `List<Long>`.** Upstream's `mihonapp/mihon#3589` declares its two keys through `getLongArray`, which exists here too, so both compile. The deciding factor is `CategoryIdPreferences`: its whole purpose is that one declaration makes every cleanup path (category delete, the scrub migration, the restore remap) pick a key up, and those paths are typed for `Set<String>`. Adopting upstream's type would need a parallel bucket and hand-maintained coverage, which is the drift that class exists to prevent. The cost is that upstream's preference declaration and its filter-sheet UI are not taken verbatim; the SQL, repository and interactor halves of that commit still are.
- **The category filter reaches every lane of whichever surface is rendered, and each rendered surface owns its own selection** (ruled 2026-08-08, revised the same day). The first half settles what the old ruling was really about: leaving the filter on the updated lane would mean the combined modes filter one of three lanes by category, which is not a category filter. The second half is ruling 10's argument, which the first cut of this ruling had not absorbed: with the combined tab off, Updates and History are two independent-looking tabs, so one shared selection would have each silently move the other, and would leave History constrained by something set elsewhere with no control of its own until step 8. The price is worth stating whole: nine preference keys instead of three; `historyView.sq` gets a predicate upstream does not have, which propagates into `HistoryRepository` and `GetHistory`; History gets a sheet it has never had and that sheet must become mode-scoped a step earlier than planned; each picker collapses to one section over the union; and the consequence below has to be accepted.
- **A category include filter hides non-library entries from History, and that is accepted.** `historyView` has no `favorite = 1` gate, unlike `updatesView`, so History shows entries that were read but never added to the library. Those have no category rows, so they read as uncategorized and survive an include filter only when Default is among the included ids. This is what a category filter means once the feed is not library-gated, and special-casing non-library rows would be a rule nobody could predict from the UI. It is recorded because it is invisible until someone hits it.
- **The picker's clobber is fixed in the shared component, not worked around per surface** (ruled 2026-08-08). A replace-write behind a subset picker is a defect wherever it appears, and the component now backs three surfaces, so fixing the mechanism once is smaller than teaching each caller to be careful and it retires the class of bug rather than an instance. It also means each surface picks its display scope on the merits instead of being forced into the union by a correctness constraint. This touches the library, which is a different surface, and it is included on the "refactor alongside the change that motivates it" rule rather than as a cleanup pass.
- **The master toggle survives** (ruled 2026-08-08, reversing an earlier recommendation to retire it). Upstream derives "filtering is on" from set emptiness and has no toggle, which was the argument for dropping ours. Two things outweigh it: the library carries the identical master switch, so dropping this one makes the same control behave differently on two surfaces, and the checkbox is drawn by the shared `CategoryFilterRow`, which would have to grow a no-master mode to lose it. The convenience it buys, parking a selection without applying it, is real on its own terms.
- **A shared filter set inherits the library's lossy restore.** `PreferenceRestorer` remaps shared sets on the manga pass only, because novel categories do not exist yet at preference-restore time, so a backup's novel ids in the pair are dropped. The library accepted that on the grounds that a filter is cheaply re-picked; the same reasoning applies here, and it is a small regression against today's novel keys, which restore intact.
- **The chip is one value per rendered surface, not one per mode and not one for the app.** Inside the Recents tab there is one chip strip over one list, so one key; switching mode must not move it. With the preference off there are two independent-looking tabs, so their two existing keys stay. The first cut of this ruling collapsed all three into one key and was wrong for exactly the users the preference default is protecting.
- **Row identity and target chapter are separate fields, the target rule is the lane's, and resolution is lazy.** Conflating identity and target is what makes a title-centric feed impossible to add later without reworking both screens. Picking one global rule would silently change what tapping a history row does. Resolving eagerly would put a chapter query per row on every emission.
- **The digest drops Yokai's twelve-hour upload tiebreak** (ruled 2026-08-08). It orders new chapters by upload date when two were fetched within twelve hours of each other. Novels have no upload date to order by, and unifying the two feeds on one column was already rejected for hiding novel updates, so the rule would silently apply to half a mixed section. The cost is that a batch of chapters fetched together sits in fetch order rather than reading order, which the target-chapter rule already handles on tap.
- **The two Mihon models stay live.** The takeover stops at orchestration, and this surface's upstream churn is the lowest of any taken over so far, so there is no case for absorbing engine files.
- **The assembly kernel is pinned before it is wired.** The library did this and it is the only part of a takeover that can be tested without a device, since the models resolve Injekt at construction.
- **The kernel orders totally, and the collapse scope is not its decision** (ruled 2026-08-08). A partial order over a concatenation of lanes leaves equal-timestamp rows in whatever order the lanes emitted, which shuffles between emissions for no visible reason; the tie-break runs lane, then content type, then entry id, and a read leads at the same instant because it is the one thing the user did rather than something that happened to their library. Keeping the collapse scope out of the kernel is what stops the digest inheriting Yokai's dropped row, and it costs one extra call at each policy.
- **A single-type chip does not ignore a filter it cannot satisfy** (ruled 2026-08-08). Asked whether including a manga-only category should leave the Novels chip unfiltered rather than empty, the answer is no: that is a silent no-op of a filter for one content type, which is the thing the capability-slot rule forbids and which the scanlator switch is being fixed for. It also inverts the result instead of explaining it, and it has a cliff, since adding one universal category to the same selection would make the filter apply again with nothing on screen to say why. The real complaint, an unexplained empty screen, is answered by the two items below.
- **An empty feed says whether a filter caused it, and offers a way in** (built in step 1). The surface previously showed the same message whether nothing was new or a filter had hidden everything, which sends the user looking for chapters that are behind their own filter. This also covers the chapter-state filters, which the rejected proposal above would not have.
- **The picker marks a category restricted to one library**, on a second line in the secondary colour rather than in the label, since one selection now spans both libraries and picking a manga-only row while thinking about novels is otherwise invisible. Universal rows carry no line: a label on every row marks nothing. This needed an optional subtitle on Mihon's `TriStateItem`, declared before `onClick` so existing trailing-lambda call sites keep binding correctly.
- **Tracker push stays where it is, on the details screens** (ruled 2026-08-08). Marking read from this surface will not push progress, which matches both these screens today and upstream's Updates tab. The swipe affordance makes the surface a plausible place to finish a chapter, so the alternative was arguable, but it would put a remote write behind a gesture on a feed built for skimming, and diverge from Mihon for no parity gain.

## Gotchas worth knowing before starting

- **The home-screen widget reads the unfiltered novel updates query.** Two of its three callers are the widget, so change that query's signature and the widget silently starts filtering. Add a filtered query beside it instead. The manga widgets are safe: they call the unfiltered `subscribe` path, not `getRecentUpdatesWithFilters`.
- **All four models hardcode their `RecentsSurface`**; only the added lane takes it from the adapter it was constructed with. An engine built for the combined Recents tab therefore filters its read and updated lanes by the Updates and History selections until the cutover parameterizes or dissolves those models. Deliberate (owner, 2026-08-08): patching two Mihon files early buys nothing while the tab does not exist.
- **The in-memory preference store's `changes()` emits nothing at all**, where the Android one emits the current value on collect. So a preference-driven flow can be given a starting value in a unit test (`stateIn` seeds from `get()`), but never a second one, and a test that expects a preference write to propagate silently passes on the initial value instead. Pin such a rule as a pure function, not through the store.
- **Merging the two flows couples their emission rates.** Each content type has its own download-cache tick, and one completed download would re-run assembly over both types' rows. The library's guards are unequal (the manga combine has two `distinctUntilChanged`, the novel one has none, and each debounces search on its own timer), so hoist the query above both and do not let the weaker guard become the shared one.
- **The merge group repository rejects the mixed content type on every method that takes one**, sixteen of eighteen; the two that take no content type (`getGroup`, `dissolveGroup`) are keyed by group id instead. Merge groups are per type and only the providers reach the repository, each with its own hardcoded type; assembly must never pass the chip down.
- **Each tab gets its own `ViewModelStore`, and it survives tab switches.** `TabNavigator` builds a real `Navigator`, the per-screen `AndroidScreenLifecycleOwner` is keyed by `Screen.key` (the class name by default), and `TabNavigator` passes `disposeSteps = false` so switching tabs does not dispose the outgoing one. Two tabs resolving the same ViewModel type therefore get two separate instances, which is what makes a per-tab chip possible without any trick, and equally what makes an ungated engine run its queries twice. Verified against the voyager 2.2.21 sources; the rule file used to claim the opposite and has been corrected.
- **Removing the current tab from the tab list fails quietly, not loudly.** Tab selection compares classes, so the orphan tab keeps rendering with nothing highlighted. Whether the tab also survives process death is a separate question about how Voyager saves the navigator; verify that path with an actual process kill rather than a preference flip.
- **`TabOptions.index` is read nowhere in this repo**, so index collisions when the tab set changes shape are cosmetic.
- **The unread badge's counter is already shared**: the novel update job increments the same preference the manga job does, and the Updates screen resets it. Only the badge's tab-type key is manga-specific.
- **History is already one row per entry in SQL.** What it does not collapse is a series merged across sources, which are distinct rows. Do not "fix" the per-entry deduplication that is already there.
- **The two feed bounds use different columns on purpose.** Manga bounds on chapter upload date, novels on fetch date, because many light-novel sources leave upload date at zero. Unifying them would hide novel updates.
- **Moving a preference between `CategoryIdPreferences` buckets changes which delete paths scrub it.** The category-delete routing picks buckets by content type, so a key that moves from the per-type buckets into the shared one is scrubbed by every content type's delete instead of one. That is the intent here, but it is a behaviour change, not a reshuffle.
- **`UpdatesViewModel`'s `// RK` islands are not all category-filter islands.** Twelve markers and seven islands, of which three are delimited; the third opens with the category filter and closes after the custom-info overlay, so moving filtering into SQL rebuilds that island around the overlay rather than clearing it. Count them case-sensitively, or `work` and `Network` match too.
- **No Compose UI tests exist**, so a bulk deletion of composables cannot be caught by the suite. Verify a cutover by function inventory and by walking the consumers of every state field that moves.
- **Adding a parameter to a Compose component can rebind an existing trailing lambda.** Putting `subtitle` after `onClick` on `TriStateItem` silently pointed a caller's trailing lambda at the new parameter. The compiler caught it, but the same shape on a same-typed parameter would not fail. Add to the middle, not the end.
- **The `date_added` column is not null on manga and nullable on novels**, so the recently-added queries coalesce on the novel side, in the predicate and the ordering both. Without it a novel added before the column existed drops out of the lane rather than sorting last.
- **Names this plan deliberately does not fix, and the first step has to choose.** The four new preference key strings and their dead-key constants; where the master toggle and the chip key live, given that `CategoryIdPreferences` holds only the two id sets; the new engine's filename, which step 8 writes into two manifest `Replacement` cells; the `.sqm` number; and the persisted mode enum step 10's shortcuts switch on, which no earlier step creates. Pick them once, early, and use them consistently.
