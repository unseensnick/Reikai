# Local series with volumes

## Goal

A local series can hold several volumes and show them as one series: its chapters under **Vol. N** headers on the details screen and in the reader's chapter sheet, with volume chips (All, Vol. 1, Vol. 2, ...) that show one volume, remembered per series. Volumes are put together at import, by converting an existing merge of local volumes, or later from the library or details, and taken apart the same way, without reimporting. Requested in unseensnick/Reikai#86 (items 2 and 6).

## Why

Merging separate local volumes is the only way to keep them together today, and a merge lines chapters up across its sources by number. Volumes reuse numbers and names ("Chapter 1", "Afterword"), so the merged All view hides some chapters and swaps others. Inside one series a chapter's address already names its volume's file and chapters are numbered across the whole series, so nothing collides; what is missing is the volume as something the user can see, order and act on.

## Approach

**The volume on a chapter** is read from its address, which names the EPUB for a novel and the volume folder for manga, so both types answer it by one rule and a refresh cannot lose it; nothing is stored per chapter. Local manga reads volume folders already: a subfolder holding chapter archives or chapter folders is a volume (`Vol 01/Ch 001.cbz`), a folder of only pictures stays one chapter, and the volume leads the chapter's name, as a local novel's does. A volumes table keyed by series and volume holds what the address cannot: the order and the label, filled in listing order, the label defaulting to the book's series number or the folder's number. `novel_chapters.page` is not the volume: the details screen reads any page label as a paged source and shows one page at a time (the spike hit this). The name loses its `Vol. N - ` prefix under a header.

**Showing it.** The shared chapter list (`entryChapterItems`) gains a third row kind beside `Chapter` and `Missing`, a `Volume` header, emitted where the label changes. A header shows the label, the volume's cover, its read count ("3/14"), and actions to mark the volume read or unread and to select it (`EntrySelection.toggleBlock`). Headers only make sense while each volume's chapters stay together, so they show under the source-order sort and hide under the others. The reader's chapter sheet (`ReaderChapterListDialog`) gets the same header row. Volume chips reuse `MergeSourceChips`' look over a neutral row, as a cell of their own, since a source chip also changes the header, web actions and download scope. The picked chip is stored per series (`local_volume_pick`). A header's cover is a manga volume folder's `cover` or `folder` picture, or its only picture, and a novel book's own cover (`LocalVolumeCovers`); other loose pictures in a manga volume folder read as an Extras chapter, the folder itself opened as a folder of pictures, numbered just below the volume's first chapter so it heads the volume under either sort. Collapsing a volume is still to come. Chips follow volume order whatever the chapter sort; the first reads **All volumes**.

**Reading across volumes.** The chapter after a volume's last is the next volume's first, as now, and the transition already names the volume: both readers' transitions show the chapter's own name, which carries the volume in front for both types ("Vol 02 - Ch 003", "Vol. 2 - Prologue"), so it needs no field of its own (checked on a device).

**Reading them safely.** A series, volume or chapter folder that cannot be listed fails the refresh instead of reading as empty, which would delete every chapter row with its read state; a local novel already failed it. A picked folder's listing can come back empty or short without failing, so a manga series that lists no chapter at all fails the refresh too, as a local novel with no book does, and a move deletes only the entries its copy holds. A folder inside a volume counts as a chapter only when it holds a picture or an archive, and folders a system keeps (`__MACOSX`, `@eaDir`, the recycle bins) are never chapters, so a chapter of pictures holding one stays a chapter. A folder is a volume only when it holds a chapter archive or book, or no pages of its own beside its cover, so a chapter of pages holding a folder of pictures (credits, a group's extras) stays one chapter, as upstream reads it; a volume made of picture-folder chapters with loose pictures beside them therefore reads as one chapter.

**Putting volumes together and apart.** One kernel moves local files and keeps everything that points at them (`moveLocalFiles`, `LocalChapterMoveRepository`): it copies every file and checks it, then in one database transaction writes a novel's new address, moves each chapter row to its new series and address with what is keyed by owner and address (number corrections, copy marks, a manga's history row) and moves the volumes' saved order and names, and only then deletes the originals; a failed copy or transaction deletes the copies and changes nothing. A saved order or name already at a volume's new key belongs to files that are gone, and is replaced, for a move and for Rename files alike. What runs after the commit (the library changes and listing the series again) only tidies around it, so a failure there is logged and the Undo still stands: a novel left with no book cannot be listed, and keeps its old list. Rows keyed by chapter id (progress, sessions, words, a novel's history) follow their row. Series that are combined into another leave the library, their trackers and categories with them; their volumes' saved names come along, and their saved order too when the kept series has one, placed after it. Every move offers Undo, as merging does (`LocalVolumeMove`): the files are copied back through the same kernel without what the move's folders gained since (a relist writes a cover into a split-out series), the rows move back, the entries that left rejoin and a split-out series leaves; the old folders and covers are deleted only once the Undo window closes, or at once when the screen is left before the move reports back, since no Undo is offered then. Combine as volumes dissolves the merge, combines, and merges again after a failed combine or an Undo as one step on the app scope, so leaving the screen part way never leaves the merge dissolved with nothing combined. A manga series with loose chapters beside its volume folders brings those in as one more volume, as the kept series does with its own. On top of it:
- **Import** groups a batch by its books' series, and each card's **Add to series** combines it with another card or adds it to a series in the library. Only a folder takes files in, so a loose book is not offered (Add volumes on its details screen takes others in), and an import never deletes a folder it did not make. Switching a card between novel and comic finds its match again in that type's folder. A card whose title matches a series already there says "Adds to *X*", with **Keep separate** to make it a new series ("*X* (2)" on disk) instead; nothing joins silently. A book is named `<Series> - <label>.epub` as Rename files names it, its label its own number or else its place; a comic keeps its name, which names its chapter.
- **Convert** a merge whose members are all local into one series, from Manage sources (**Combine as volumes**, shown only then): the group dissolves and the entry the screen is open on keeps the rest as its volumes; Undo moves them back out and restores the group as captured.
- **Library selection**: with only local series of one type selected, **Combine as volumes** takes Merge's place in the bar, with Merge kept in the overflow; **Add to series** picks a target. A mixed selection keeps Merge.
- **Details**: **Add volumes** and **Split out volumes**, from ⋮. Add volumes is offered on the series that receives the books, where people look for it: it ticks any number of the other local series of that type and moves them in. Split out takes any number of ticked volumes into one new series, named "<Series> - <first label>" until edited, so a mistaken combine is undone in one step; one manga volume becomes the series folder itself, several keep their folders. Split out and Reorder volumes show each volume's file or folder name under its label wherever the two differ, since two combined series can share a label. A volume chip's own menu is still to come. **Reorder volumes** and renaming a volume's label are a dialog from ⋮ over `local_volume`, which both local sources read when they list, so the source order, a novel's chapter numbers and the reader follow it after a relist; a manga sorted by chapter number keeps its numbers, which come from its files.
- Every one of these first shows what will move and how files will be renamed.

**Names.** Files on disk follow the names shown only when asked: **Rename files** (⋮, a local series of its own) names the folder after the displayed title, each manga volume folder after its label, and each book `<Series> - <label>.epub`, since a book file is handled away from its folder. A preview lists every old and new name; a name a folder or another series' row already holds gets "(2)" with a way to choose another, and a chosen name becomes the series' Edit info title (`SetEntryTitle`, keeping its other overrides), so title and files stay in step for both types, since a local novel's title comes from its book and a manga's from its folder. Edit info (once the title changes) and Reorder volumes (once a volume is renamed) show **Also rename files**, ticked, which opens the same preview on Save or Close; a snackbar was easy to miss. A local novel offers no Export as EPUB, in its menu or for an all-local library selection, since its books are EPUB files already. It renames in place, then writes the address, the chapters' addresses and the volumes' saved order, labels and picked chip in one transaction, and puts the names back if that fails; Undo renames back. The novel details screen follows its series by id once found, since its address changes. The import's `%02d - ` prefix gives way to this naming.

**Duplicates.** Nothing is decided silently. Volumes that show one label both show ("Vol. 3", "Vol. 3 (2)", by chip order) under a notice above the chips until the user keeps them, **Keep all** saving each later one's shown name as its label so they no longer clash, or deletes some, which opens **Delete volumes** (also in ⋮ for a local series of its own) on just the clashing ones. Delete lists each volume with its file and read count, asks to confirm since it cannot be undone, deletes the files for good (a manga volume folder with all it holds), forgets their saved order, names and picked chip, and lists the series again. An import clash, a file the series already holds by size, shows on its card with Replace, Keep both or Skip, Keep both chosen until changed; Replace copies beside the old file and takes its name only once whole, so its chapters keep their read state. The import's silent name-and-size skip is gone.

**Merged sources that have volumes** are not being built: a watch item in [parked.md](../parked.md), revived by a request on GitHub. The design kept for that case: the stitch pairs chapters by volume and chapter rather than number alone, a picked local source gets a second chip row (the source row's first chip reading **All sources**, the volume row's **All volumes**), and in the merged All list each chapter carries its volume in its subtitle; a source without volumes gives no basis to pair a local volume's chapters, so they stay unpaired.

## Open questions

- **A volume chip's own menu** (rename, split out or delete one volume from its chip): build it, or keep those actions in ⋮ only, where they are now.
- **A combined series' own `details.json` and `ComicInfo.xml`** go with its folder once the Undo window closes, as its cover does, since the folder then holds nothing else. Either may have been written by hand; carrying them into the kept series, or keeping the folder (an empty series in Local browse), are the alternatives.
- **Deleting every volume of a series**: it keeps its old chapter list, since a series with no files cannot be listed (both types). Remove it from the library then, or offer that in the Delete volumes dialog.
- **Merged sources that have volumes**: waits for a request on GitHub ([parked.md](../parked.md)); the design to use if it is revived is in Approach.

## Decisions & tradeoffs

- **One series, not a better merge.** A merge's job is to line up the same chapters from different places; volumes are different chapters, so the fix is to stop merging them.
- **Duplicates are never resolved automatically** (owner ruling): both copies show until the user chooses.
- **Combine as volumes replaces Merge only for an all-local selection** (owner ruling), with Merge still in the overflow for two local copies of one series.
- **Reorder is a sheet from the ⋮ menu, not a mode** (owner ruling): dragging headers inline would fight the chapter long press.
- **A manga volume is a folder of chapter files** (owner ruling): the local manga source reads one level of volume folders inside a series folder, in an RK island, since that is how volume-split comics are usually stored; a CBZ stays one chapter.
- **Volume order lives in a Reikai table, carried in backups** (owner ruling), not in the folder's `details.json`, which is edited by hand and would reorder silently when stale.
- **A chapter's volume is read from its address, not stored** (owner ruling): manga's `SChapter` is the extension contract and has no field for it, while both types' addresses already name the volume's file or folder.
